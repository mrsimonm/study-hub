## Ecogeographical rules | 4 min
**Ecogeographical rules** are patterns that link body size, shape or colour to broad environmental gradients.

| Rule | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **Bergmann's rule** | larger bodies in colder climates |
| **Allen's rule** | shorter extremities (ears, limbs) in cold regions |
| **Gloger's rule** | darker pigmentation in warm, humid areas |

:::ana Memory hook
Bergmann = Bigger. Allen = Appendages. Gloger = Glow (colour).
:::

## The island rule | 5 min
The **island rule** (Foster's rule, after Bristol Foster; Leigh Van Valen is also linked to it): compared with mainland relatives, **small species get bigger** and **large species get smaller** on islands.

- **Insular gigantism**: small animals get bigger, presumably because there is less predation and competition.
- **Insular dwarfism**: large animals get smaller, linked to limited resources and relaxed predation.

:::teach Is it real?
A **meta-analysis** by Lokatis & Jeschke (2018) found that author groups disagree on whether the rule holds. Phylogenetic and non-phylogenetic models give different results. Synthesis: the rule **generally holds**, but is **context- and lineage-dependent**, and **methods matter**.
:::

## Reading a paper | 3 min
The abstract is a mini version of the paper:

| Abstract part | Section |
|---|---|
| What is known | Introduction |
| Data & statistics | Methods |
| Main results | Results |
| Interpretations | Discussion |

Example talk (Bulla et al.): song complexity in songbirds was measured as the **number of different note types in a sequence of 50 notes**, from about 18,500 recordings of 4,940 species. Extreme complexity comes from vocal learning, ecological context and evolutionary history **together**, not from one driver.
