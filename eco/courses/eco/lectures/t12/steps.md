## From new dawn to new standard | 4 min
**Citizen science**: members of the public take part in real research.

- 1600s: amateurs built early science. 1800s: science became a profession and the public was excluded.
- **1900**: the **Christmas Bird Count** (proposed by Frank Chapman) began modern mass participation.
- **Silvertown 2009**, "A new dawn": modern citizen science is open to **all, not a privileged few**. Drivers: technology, the public as a free resource, funders demanding outreach.
- **Fraisl et al. 2022**, "new standard": now a standard method, with design, quality control and ethics written down.

## Data: GBIF, eBird and bias | 5 min
- **eBird**: bird observations with **explicit effort** (start time, duration).
- **GBIF**: aggregates occurrence records and sampling-event datasets. Volunteers supply **50 %+** of its data.

:::trap Volunteer data are biased
More records near dense populations and roads, in daytime and at weekends, and from a few heavy contributors. So: (i) **filter for quality** (complete checklists with bounded effort; sampling events over bare occurrences) and (ii) **model the observation process** (effort covariates, occupancy–detection models).
:::

**Quality assurance** = before and during collection (training, protocols). **Quality control** = after (outlier flags, photo checks).

## Good practice and participation | 5 min
**Ten Principles of Citizen Science** (ECSA, 2015): genuine participation, a genuine science outcome, mutual benefits, multiple stages, **participant feedback**, limitations and biases acknowledged, open data, proper credit, evaluation, and legal and ethical issues.

**Haklay typology** (2013):
1. **Crowdsourcing**: volunteers collect data.
2. Distributed intelligence: volunteers also interpret.
3. **Participatory science**: volunteers help define the problem.
4. **Extreme citizen science**: co-design at every stage.

Unlike Arnstein's ladder (bad → good), every Haklay level has value, and people can move between levels.

Examples: Spipoll (photograph every insect on one plant for 20 minutes), MammalWeb (camera traps), and apps such as Merlin, BirdNET, Pl@ntNet and iNaturalist.
