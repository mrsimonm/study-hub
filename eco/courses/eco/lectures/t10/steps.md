## The crisis and its drivers | 5 min
**Biodiversity crisis**: global declines in species, populations and ecosystem integrity, driven by human habitat change, exploitation, climate change, pollution and invasions.

:::trap Direct vs indirect
- **Direct drivers**: measurable pressures that immediately change ecosystems: habitat conversion, harvesting, pollution, introductions, greenhouse-gas emissions.
- **Indirect drivers**: the social, economic, demographic, technological and institutional processes behind them. Human activity is behind the direct drivers.
:::

## Jaureguiberry et al. 2022 | 3 min
A synthesis of the direct drivers of recent global biodiversity loss: **45,162 studies** screened down to **154**. Drivers were compared across the **terrestrial**, **freshwater** and **marine** realms and across world regions (the Americas, Europe and Central Asia, Africa, Asia and the Pacific). Biodiversity is tracked with **Essential Biodiversity Variables**.

## Bar-On et al. 2018: Earth's biomass | 5 min
A **biomass census** in **gigatons of carbon** (Gt C, water excluded).

| Group | Gt C |
|---|---|
| Plants | 450 |
| Bacteria | 70 |
| Fungi | 12 |
| Archaea | 7 |
| Protists | 4 |
| Animals | 2 |

Within animals: livestock **0.1**, humans **0.06**, wild mammals **0.007**, wild birds **0.002**.

**Biomass reallocation**: vertebrate biomass has shifted from wild species to humans and livestock. Plants are down about 50 %, and wild mammals are down about **82.5 %** (land −85 %, marine −80 %).
