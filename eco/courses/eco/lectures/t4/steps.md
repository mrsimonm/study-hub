## What an Allee effect is | 4 min
Normally crowding hurts: more neighbours means more **competitors**. But neighbours are also **partners**. With too few of them, life gets harder.

**Allee effect**: the smaller the population, the lower each individual's chance of surviving or reproducing.

Mechanisms from Group 2:
- **Mates**: sea urchins
- **Safety**: island marmots
- **Teamwork**: African wild dogs
- **Changing habitat**: bark beetles

## Component vs demographic | 4 min
| | Component | Demographic |
|---|---|---|
| What drops at low density | **one** fitness measure | growth per individual of the **whole population** |
| Example | African wild dogs: pup survival rises with pack size (up to ≈12) | Vancouver Island marmot: population grows more slowly when small |

:::trap Not the same thing
A component effect does not always add up to a demographic one: other components can compensate.
:::

## Strong vs weak, and rescue | 5 min
- **Weak**: small populations grow more slowly, but there is no hard threshold.
- **Strong**: below a **threshold**, more die than are born, so the population heads to extinction. Gypsy moth colonies below ≈107 males tend to shrink on their own.

**Rescue effect**: if patches are connected, immigrants top up a small population. Isolated patches have no one to rescue them. Models show "Allee pits": connectivity is not always simply good.

## The evidence and the answer | 4 min
- **Stephens et al. 1999** defined the terms (no new data).
- **Muir et al. 2024**: meta-analysis of **62 experimental studies**.
- Same gap 25 years apart: theory is about demographic effects, experiments measure component effects.
- The stricter the test, the rarer the evidence.

:::idea Group 2's verdict
**Not a curiosity, but a fundamental driver.** Allee effects decide the fate of small, declining and harvested populations, often before we can detect them.
:::
