## What is a species? | 5 min
- **Biological species concept** (Ernst Mayr): interbreeding natural populations, reproductively isolated from others. A horse and a donkey make a mule, but the mule is infertile, so they stay separate species.
- **Morphological**: distinct phenotypes.
- **Phylogenetic**: smallest diagnosable monophyletic lineages.

:::trap Where the biological concept fails
Asexual organisms and **prokaryotes** (binary fission), **geographic isolation** (interbreeding can't be observed), and **hybridisation** (common in plants, some ducks). So each field adapts its own definition, and different concepts give **different counts**.
:::

## Estimating the number | 5 min
Older methods (each with limitations):
- **Body size distribution** (Robert May): 10–50 million animals.
- **Latitudinal gradient** (Raven): 3–5 million large organisms.
- **Known-to-unknown ratios** (Hodkinson & Casson): 1.84–2.57 million insects.
- **Author accumulation curves**: 13–18 % of plants still undiscovered.

**Mora et al. 2011**: assumed higher taxa had levelled off, fitted a regression and **extrapolated** → about **8.75 million** species. To describe them all: ~$364 billion and 303,000 full-time taxonomists.

## Why it's so hard (Wiens 2023) | 4 min
1. **Limited DNA data**: metabarcoding and sequencing reveal far more diversity.
2. **Cryptic diversity**: e.g. *Loxodonta africana* vs *L. cyclotis* (two elephant species).
3. **Prokaryotes underestimated**: currently 2.2–4.3 million OTUs; they may be a major driver of total richness.
4. **Assumption of stagnation** is wrong: >700 new genera are described each year.

Why count at all? To locate small-ranged, threatened species, to calculate extinction rates, to track climate change, and for education.
