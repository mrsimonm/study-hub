## Top-down vs bottom-up | 4 min
- **Top-down control**: predator pressure regulates prey numbers.
- **Bottom-up control**: food availability, food quality and plant productivity set how many herbivores there can be.

**Predation** (glossary): any interaction that cuts prey's future survival or reproduction to zero. It includes parasitoids, parasitic castration, herbivory and seed predation. Its effects can be **lethal** (killing) or **non-lethal** (stress, behaviour change).

A **trophic cascade**: a change at one level ripples down (predators → herbivores → plants), with alternating ups and downs.

## Krebs et al. 1995: the experiment | 4 min
Forest plots with four treatments: control, **food addition**, **predator exclusion** (fences against mammals), and **both**. Plus separate fertilised plots.

- Predator exclusion raised density → **top-down** effect.
- Food raised density → **bottom-up** effect.
- **Both together ≈ 11×**: the biggest effect. Food and predation **interact**.

:::trap The decline still came
Birds of prey could still hunt, and hares could move out of the fenced plots. The decline was delayed, not prevented.
:::

## Oli et al. 2026: the demography | 4 min
43 years of data (1977–2020), more than 20,000 captures, **capture-mark-recapture** models.

- Survival is higher in the increase phase.
- Declines come from **reduced survival, mainly before weaning**.

:::idea Verdict
Predation strongly influences hare survival, and food and predation interact. Still unclear: why reproduction changes, and why populations stay low for years. **Predation plays a major role, but food and demography are also essential.**
:::
