## Lecture structure · Pre-Darwinian: Lamarck · Darwin & Wallace · Mendel | 2 min
- **History**: pre-Darwinian, Darwin, Mendel, modern synthesis, evo-devo
- **Microevolution**: natural selection, mutation, gene flow, genetic drift
- **Macroevolution**: speciation, extinction, evolution of life on Earth


### Pre-Darwinian: Lamarck
- **Jean-Baptiste Lamarck (1744–1829)**
1. **Tendency toward perfection**: e.g. birds kept trying to fly until their wings developed; giraffes stretched their necks to reach treetops
2. **Inheritance of acquired traits**: a giraffe's offspring would be born with a longer neck (this is wrong)


### Darwin & Wallace
- **Charles Darwin (1809–1882)**, English naturalist: **all species descended over time from common ancestors** (p. 4)
- **1831–1836 voyage on HMS Beagle**: **Galápagos (finches)**, South America (fossils); puzzled by the geographical distribution of wildlife and fossils → **1838** conceived the theory of **natural selection** (p. 4)
- **1858**: joint preliminary publication with **Alfred Russel Wallace** (p. 5)
- **1859**: ***On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection*** (p. 5)
- With the **modern synthesis (1930s–40s)**, natural selection became central to evolutionary theory, now the **unifying concept of the life sciences** (p. 5)


### Mendel
- **Gregor Johann Mendel (1822–1884)**: Augustinian friar and **abbot of St Thomas' Abbey in Brno**; worked with **peas (Fabaceae)**
- **Mendelian inheritance**: laws proposed **1865 and 1866**, **rediscovered in 1900** → core of **classical genetics**

## Modern synthesis & beyond · Micro- vs macroevolution · Natural selection | 3 min
- **Modern synthesis (1920s–1930s)**: joined **natural selection + population genetics** (based on Mendelian inheritance) into one theory for all biology (Julian Huxley; Dobzhansky with model *Drosophila*) (p. 7)
- **Theodosius Dobzhansky (1973)**: ***"Nothing in biology makes sense except in the light of evolution."*** (p. 7)
- **Evo-devo** (evolutionary developmental biology): how evolution acts on development; **Hox genes** (regulatory) (p. 8)
- **Extended evolutionary synthesis**: adds non-genetic inheritance, i.e. **epigenetics**, parental effects, ecological and cultural inheritance (**memes**) (p. 8)


### Micro- vs macroevolution
- **Microevolution**: small genetic changes **within a species/population**, e.g. spread of a mutation or change in the frequency of one allele due to selection; **impossible without genetic variation** (p. 9)
- **Macroevolution**: long-term, large-scale changes, e.g. **formation of new species** (p. 9)
- Microevolution = change in heritable traits over generations due to **natural selection, mutation, gene flow and genetic drift**; also called **"descent with modification"** (p. 10)
- In sexual organisms, **meiosis + recombination** make offspring genetically different from parents (variation: snail shell colours, flower colour, F1 pink from red × white) (p. 11)


### Natural selection
- **Three conditions** (p. 12):
- ↳ **Natural variability** of a trait in a population
- ↳ The trait must be **heritable**
- ↳ The trait must lead to **differential reproduction**
- **Adaptation** = a heritable trait enabling organisms to **survive AND reproduce** (p. 12)
- **Steps** (p. 13):
- ↳ **Mutation** (random) adds genetic variation
- ↳ Mutations change the **phenotype**
- ↳ Natural selection acts on the phenotype
- ↳ Better-suited individuals have **more offspring** (contribute more to the gene pool)
- ↳ The population's **gene pool changes** over time
- ↳ **Speciation** may follow with geographic and reproductive isolation

## Types of selection · Responses to environmental change | 2 min
- **Stabilising**: **intermediate forms favoured**; extreme alleles eliminated. Examples: **human birth weight**, **clutch size** in birds (p. 14)
- **Disruptive**: **both extremes favoured**; intermediates eliminated; **rare**. Example: **bill size in African finches** (black-bellied seedcracker: large and small beaks for two food types) (p. 15)
- **Directional**: allele frequencies **shift consistently in one direction**. Example: butterfly colour (p. 16)
- ↳ **Peppered moth (*Biston betularia*)**: **industrial melanism**, more dark forms in UK populations because of **air pollution** in the Industrial Revolution; mechanism: **bird predation** (somewhat controversial: the moths rest mostly on branches) (p. 17)
- **Natural vs sexual selection**: environmental factors vs **reproduction** (p. 18)
- ↳ **Sexual selection**: **female choice** or **male–male competition**
- ↳ **Giraffe's long neck = result of sexual selection** (males fight with their necks), **not an adaptation to tall trees** (p. 18)
- ↳ Birds: **males more colourful** than females (female choice), e.g. **blue peafowl (*Pavo cristatus*)** (p. 19–20)


### Responses to environmental change
- A population can **migrate**, be **already adapted**, or **die (local extinction)**
- **Natural selection can only act on alleles already present**; the environment does **not create** favourable heritable traits
- New alleles arise by **mutation**

## Other mechanisms · Macroevolution: speciation | 2 min
- **Mutation**: change in DNA structure; adds diversity; **may or may not be adaptive, depending on the environment** (p. 22)
- **Gene flow**: flow of alleles by **emigration and immigration** (p. 23)
- **Genetic drift**: **random** change in allele frequencies **by chance**; on its own leads to **loss of genetic diversity** (example: *Harmonia axyridis* colour forms); **strongest in small populations** (p. 24–25)
- ↳ **Bottleneck effect**: sudden reduction in population size (e.g. environmental catastrophe) → changed allele proportions (p. 26, p. 28)
- ↳ **Founder effect**: loss of variation when a **new population is founded by very few individuals** from a larger one (migration); e.g. distribution of the **O blood group** in native human populations (p. 27–28)
- **Domestication**: dog breeds (Chihuahua from wolf), **artificial selection, not natural selection** (p. 29)


### Macroevolution: speciation
- **Speciation** = one species diverges into two or more descendant species (p. 30)
- **Allopatric speciation** (geographic separation), e.g. butterflies of the genus ***Charis*** in South America (p. 31)
- **Adaptive radiation** = **rapid diversification of a single ancestral line** (p. 32–33)
- ↳ **Galápagos finches** (Geospizidae): many beak types; **neoendemics**
- ↳ **Cichlid fishes of Lake Tanganyika**: repeated **convergence** in body and feeding morphology
- **Models of speciation** (p. 34):
- ↳ **Gradualism**: **continuous**, slow morphological change
- ↳ **Punctuated equilibrium**: long stasis with **sudden (discontinuous)** change at speciation

## Extinction & history of life · Literature | 2 min
- **Extinction** = end of an organism or a group (taxon, lineage); known from fossils (compressions, amber inclusions) (p. 35)
- **History of life as a 24-hour clock** (p. 36):
- ↳ **Origin of life 3.6–3.8 billion years ago**
- ↳ Fossils present but rare → evolution and expansion of life → fossils abundant → **plants invade land** → **insects and amphibians invade land** → **age of reptiles** → **age of mammals** (from 65 million years ago)
- ↳ **Modern humans appear about 2 seconds before midnight**; recorded history **¼ second** before midnight
- **Five mass extinctions** = loss of **> 75 % of organisms in a short time** (p. 37): **end-Ordovician, late Devonian, end-Permian, end-Triassic, end-Cretaceous** (the dinosaurs; asteroid impact)

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Evolution by natural selection (p. 12–13)
Random mutation → genetic variation → phenotypic differences → selection → better-adapted individuals reproduce more → gene pool changes → (isolation) → speciation

### Bottleneck vs founder effect (p. 26–28)
- Bottleneck: catastrophe kills most of the population → the few survivors carry a random subset of alleles
- Founder: a few individuals migrate and found a new population → a random subset of alleles
