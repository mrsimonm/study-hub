## Why a cell cycle · Interphase & gap phases | 2 min
- Cells reproduce by **duplication and division**. Main task: pass genetic information to daughter cells, which means replicating it, distributing copies, duplicating organelles and macromolecules, and coordinating growth with division (s. 2)
- Basic function: **duplicate the DNA and segregate the copies into two genetically identical daughter cells** (s. 3)
- Two major phases (s. 3):
- ↳ **S phase**: chromosome duplication (DNA **S**ynthesis)
- ↳ **M phase**: **mitosis** (nuclear division) + **cytokinesis** (cytoplasmic division)
- Cycle diagram: cell growth and chromosome replication → chromosome segregation → cell division (p. 4)


### Interphase & gap phases
- **Interphase** = time between two mitoses; cells need more time to grow than to replicate and divide → **gap phases**; the eukaryotic cycle has **4 phases: G1, S, G2, M** (s. 5)
- Gap phases let the cell grow and check its internal and external environment before S and M (s. 6)
- **G1**: unfavourable conditions → **G0** (resting); favourable → **Start point** (commitment to DNA replication). In G1 the cell checks nutrients, growth signals, cell size and DNA status; it may pass the **restriction point**, enter G0 temporarily or permanently, differentiate, or with severe damage enter **senescence or apoptosis** (new) (s. 6)
- **G2**: favourable conditions → mitosis (s. 6)

## Cell-cycle control system · Cdks & cyclins | 3 min
- Delays the next phase if conditions are bad; a series of **biochemical switches**, each launching an event **completely and irreversibly**; robust even if parts fail; adaptable to cell type and signals (s. 7)
- **Three major checkpoints (nodes)** (s. 8–9):
- ↳ **Start (late G1 / G1-S)**: cell-cycle entry, chromosome duplication. Checks environment, growth signals, DNA damage
- ↳ **G2/M transition**: entry into mitosis. Checks that **replication is complete and DNA is intact**
- ↳ **Metaphase-to-anaphase**: completion of mitosis. Checks **correct kinetochore attachment and tension between sister chromatids**
- The cycle stops at these points if a problem is detected (s. 8)


### Cdks & cyclins
- **Cyclin-dependent kinases (Cdks)**: enzymes that **phosphorylate** target proteins; their activity rises and falls through the cycle → cyclic phosphorylation that triggers the major events (s. 10)
- Cdk activity is controlled by **cyclins** (s. 10)
- (new) Kinases use the **Mg-ATP complex**; Mg²⁺ offsets the phosphate charges and orients ATP (s. 11)
- **Cyclins**: cycle of synthesis and degradation each cycle; **4 types** by the stage at which they bind Cdks; activate Cdks and direct them to specific substrates; each cyclin–Cdk complex phosphorylates different proteins (s. 12)
- ↳ **G1/S-cyclins**: trigger entry at Start; fall in S phase (s. 13)
- ↳ **S-cyclins**: bind Cdks just after Start; stimulate **chromosome duplication**; stay high until mitosis and regulate early mitotic events (s. 14)
- ↳ **M-cyclins**: activate Cdks for **entry into mitosis at G2/M**; fall in mid-mitosis (s. 15)
- ↳ **G1-cyclins**: help the G1/S cyclins (s. 16)
- **The metaphase-to-anaphase transition is NOT cyclin-dependent**; it is controlled by **APC/C (anaphase-promoting complex / cyclosome)**, a large complex of **11–13 subunits** (s. 17)
- ↳ (new) **APC/C–Cdc20** (a ubiquitin ligase) tags **securin** and mitotic cyclins for degradation → released **separase** cleaves **cohesin** → sister chromatids separate (s. 17)

## S phase · Mitosis | 4 min
- Duplicates DNA and chromosomal protein structures (s. 18)
- Challenges: (1) copy **without errors** (repair mechanisms); (2) copy **only once per cycle** (**licensing of origins**) (s. 18)
- (new) The cell separates replication "permission" (G1) from initiation (S), so an origin can't be licensed twice in one cycle (s. 18)
- **Mechanism** (s. 19): in **G1**, inactive helicase is loaded on the origin → **pre-replicative complex**; in **S**, **S-Cdk activates the helicase** → replication → helicase removed → the origin can't be reused (no helicase, no unwinding)
- At the end of S, each chromosome = **two identical sister chromatids** held along their length by **cohesin** (s. 20)
- ↳ (new) Cohesin = **ring-shaped complex of SMC proteins**; holds sisters from S phase to anaphase; also organises chromatin and helps DNA repair (s. 20)


### Mitosis
- Mitosis = separation and segregation of sister chromatids; **cytokinesis** = splitting into two cells (s. 21)
- **5 stages** (by chromosome behaviour under the microscope): **prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase** (s. 21)
1. **Prophase** (s. 22): chromosomes **condense** (via **condensin**); the **mitotic spindle** assembles outside the nucleus between the **two centrosomes**; cohesin still holds sisters
2. **Prometaphase** (s. 23): **nuclear envelope breaks down**; chromosomes attach to the spindle via **kinetochores** (protein structures on centromeres); microtubules search by polymerising/depolymerising; misattachments are corrected before anaphase
3. **Metaphase** (s. 24): chromosomes align at the **spindle equator**; sister chromatids attached to **opposite poles**; the **spindle checkpoint** checks position and correct kinetochore attachment
4. **Anaphase** (s. 25–26): sister chromatids **separate** and are pulled to opposite poles
- ↳ **Anaphase A**: **kinetochore microtubules shorten**
- ↳ **Anaphase B**: **spindle poles move apart**
- ↳ (new) Spindle-checkpoint failure → **aneuploidy**; some stem cells divide **asymmetrically** (one stays a stem cell, one differentiates) (s. 27)
5. **Telophase** (s. 28): chromosomes reach the poles and **decondense**; **nuclear envelopes re-form**; the **contractile ring** of **actin + myosin** starts dividing the cytoplasm
- **Cytokinesis** (s. 29): two daughter cells
- ↳ Animals: **contractile ring (actin + myosin II)**, furrowing/cleavage
- ↳ (new) Plants: **cell plate** (mainly **pectins**, later cellulose and hemicellulose); vesicles fuse in the cell centre to form the precursor of a new wall, with no furrowing

## Meiosis | 3 min
- Sexual reproduction needs **gametes**; meiosis = division with **reduction of chromosome number**; **one duplication followed by two rounds of segregation** (s. 30)
- ↳ **Meiosis I**: **homologous chromosomes** segregate to the poles
- ↳ **Meiosis II**: **sister chromatids** separate at anaphase
- ↳ (new) Meiotic recombination starts with **programmed DNA double-strand breaks**: the cell damages its DNA on purpose to create new allele combinations (s. 30)
- Result: **meiosis → 4 haploid daughter cells**; **mitosis → 2 diploid daughter cells** (p. 29, p. 37–38)
- **Bivalent** (s. 32): homologous chromosomes (one paternal, one maternal) tightly paired in prophase I; each has 2 sister chromatids; locked together by **homologous recombination**; exchange of large DNA portions = **crossing-over**
- ↳ **Bivalent** = paired homologous chromosomes; **tetrad** = the 4 chromatids of a bivalent; **chiasma** = visible connection of homologs from crossing-over (new)
- **Prophase I: 5 stages** (s. 33–37):
- ↳ **Leptotene**: chromosomes condense; programmed DNA breaks start; pairing and recombination begin
- ↳ **Zygotene**: **synaptonemal complex** begins to assemble; recombination events
- ↳ **Pachytene**: synaptonemal complex **complete**; homologs **synapsed along their whole length**; recombination continues; can last **several days**
- ↳ **Diplotene**: synaptonemal complex **disassembles**; chromosomes condense and shorten; crossovers visible as **chiasmata**
- ↳ **Diakinesis**: **maximum condensation**, preparation for metaphase I
- (new) In **human oocytes** a diplotene-like state can persist **for many years** (s. 38)
- In meiosis I, sister kinetochores act together and homologs go to opposite poles; cohesin along the arms is lost in anaphase I, at the centromere only in anaphase II (p. 38)

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Cell cycle (s. 5–8)
G1 → (Start checkpoint) → S → G2 → (G2/M checkpoint) → M [prophase → prometaphase → metaphase → (spindle checkpoint) → anaphase → telophase] → cytokinesis → G1 (or G0)

### Anaphase trigger (s. 17)
1. All kinetochores correctly attached
2. APC/C–Cdc20 ubiquitinates securin (and M-cyclins)
3. Securin degraded → separase freed
4. Separase cleaves cohesin
5. Sister chromatids separate

### Replication licensing (s. 18–19)
1. G1: inactive helicase loaded on the origin (pre-RC)
2. S: S-Cdk activates helicase → replication
3. Helicase removed → origin can't fire again this cycle

### Mitosis stages (s. 21–29)
1. Prophase: condensation, spindle forms
2. Prometaphase: nuclear envelope breaks, kinetochore attachment
3. Metaphase: alignment at equator
4. Anaphase: chromatids separate (A: microtubules shorten; B: poles move apart)
5. Telophase: decondensation, nuclear envelopes, contractile ring
6. Cytokinesis: ring (animals) or cell plate (plants)

### Prophase I (s. 33–37)
Leptotene → Zygotene → Pachytene → Diplotene → Diakinesis

## Compare and contrast | 3 min

|---|---|---|
| Divisions | 1 | 2 (I and II) |
| Daughter cells | 2, diploid, identical | 4, haploid, genetically different |
| Homolog pairing / crossing-over | no | yes (prophase I) |
| What separates | sister chromatids | I: homologs; II: sister chromatids |
| Purpose | growth, repair | gametes, sexual reproduction |
