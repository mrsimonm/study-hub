## What plant physiology is · Cell signalling & interactions | 3 min
- A subdiscipline of botany concerned with the **general patterns governing the life processes of plants** (p. 2)
- **5 key areas**: phytochemistry, cellular interactions, molecular & cell biology, plant morphology, environmental interactions (p. 2)
- Studies **respiration, photosynthesis, plant nutrition, accumulation of substances, biosynthesis**, plus hormone functions, tropisms, nastic movements, photoperiodism, photomorphogenesis, circadian rhythms, stress physiology, germination, dormancy, stomata and transpiration (p. 3)
- **Phytochemistry**: elements C, O, H, N, P, K, S… as in other life; plants also make **secondary metabolites** found in no other organisms, for photosynthesis, **chemical defence** (plants can't move: toxins, foul tastes and smells against herbivores, pathogens, competitors), drought survival, dormancy, attracting pollinators and seed dispersers (p. 4)
- Reproduction, flowering, sex determination, incompatibility and stress resistance are set by **molecular and genetic mechanisms** (p. 5)


### Cell signalling & interactions
- Plants are **sessile** and must respond to signals: **physical** (light, pressure, temperature) and **chemical** (phytohormones, sugars, ion gradients, pathogens); signalling errors cause disease (p. 6)
- The **cell wall** is a barrier to communication → **plasmodesmata** connect the cytosol of neighbouring cells (p. 7)
- ↳ Freely pass molecules **< 1,000 Da** (ions, amino acids, sugars, signals, transcription factors); can be regulated up to **10,000 Da** (depends e.g. on **Ca²⁺**)
- ↳ = the **symplastic** transport system; important in development and **defence against viruses**
- **Apoplastic** transport: between cell-wall microfibrils (~**4 nm**), carries water and minerals (p. 7)


## Growth & development · Tissues | 2 min
- **Polarity** from directed cell division and growth; **symmetric vs asymmetric** cell division; cellulose microfibrils laid down perpendicular, guided by the cytoskeleton (p. 9)
- **Growth**: increase in size, volume, area or weight (p. 10)
- **Differentiation**: functional specialisation (p. 10)
- **Morphogenesis**: development of shape (p. 10)
- **Development (ontogeny)**: growth + differentiation, organised in time and space (p. 10)
- **Totipotency**: a plant cell can **regenerate a whole plant** under suitable conditions (from meristematic cells) (p. 10)
- **Dedifferentiation**: regaining the ability to follow multiple developmental programmes (specialised cells rarely do this) (p. 10)


### Tissues
- Cell level: **cytoplasmic streaming** moves nutrients (e.g. *Elodea*) (p. 11)
- Tissues: **meristematic** (dividing) and **permanent** (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma) (p. 11)

| Tissue | Cells | Function | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parenchyma** | living, thin cellulose walls, large intercellular spaces, large vacuole | basic filling; **storage** parenchyma; **chlorenchyma** (photosynthesis); **aerenchyma** (air spaces, buoyancy in aquatic plants) | soft parts of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits |
| **Collenchyma** | living, elongated, walls **thickened at the corners** (cellulose/pectin), few spaces | **flexibility** + mechanical support; lets parts bend | below epidermis of leaves, young stems, leaf stalks |
| **Sclerenchyma** | **dead** at maturity, thick **lignified** walls with pits, no spaces | **rigidity** and strength | stems, around vascular bundles |

## Organs | 2 min
- Roots anchor the plant and take up minerals; leaves capture light and make nutrients; **vascular tissue** connects them (p. 13)
- **Leaf**: base, petiole, **lamina (blade)**, midrib, veins (xylem + phloem), margins (p. 14)
- **Flower**: reproductive organs; **angiosperms** = flowering plants; **stamen** (male: filament + anther); **pistil** (female: stigma + style + ovary); corolla (petals), calyx (sepals) (p. 14)
- **Root**: anchors, absorbs water and minerals, stores food (p. 14)
- **Stem**: water and minerals up, food down; storage and reproduction (p. 14)
- **Taproot vs fibrous root** (p. 15):

| Taproot | Fibrous root |
|---|---|
| one large primary root, deep | no main root, shallow |
| primary root becomes the taproot | primary root eliminated; roots develop from the stem |
| lateral roots from the main root | no single main root |
| **dicots, gymnosperms** | **monocots, most grasses** |
| can store food | never stores food |
| reaches deep water | forms a net holding surface soil |
- Side roots take up mainly nutrients and water; root hairs take up O₂, micronutrients and water (p. 15)
- Root density decreases rapidly with depth (p. 16)


## Mineral nutrition | 3 min
- Growth depends on available nutrients; deficiency → stunted growth, tissue death, **chlorosis** (yellowing, less chlorophyll) → lower yield and quality, lower biodiversity; climate change alters availability (p. 17)
- **Justus von Liebig's law of the minimum**: without an essential element the plant can't complete its life cycle (growth is limited by the scarcest nutrient) (p. 18)
- **17 essential elements** (p. 18):
- ↳ **Macronutrients**: N, P, K, Ca, S, Mg + C, O, H (C and O from air, H from water = non-mineral macronutrients)
- ↳ **Micronutrients**: Fe, B, Cl, Mn, Zn, Cu, Mo, Ni (often **enzyme cofactors**)

| Macronutrient | Uptake form | Role |
|---|---|---|
| N | NO₃⁻, NH₄⁺ | nucleic acids, proteins, hormones |
| O | O₂, H₂O | organic compounds |
| C | CO₂ | organic compounds |
| H | H₂O | organic compounds |
| K | K⁺ | cofactor in protein synthesis, **water balance** |
| Ca | Ca²⁺ | membrane synthesis and stability |
| **Mg** | Mg²⁺ | **essential for chlorophyll** |
| P | H₂PO₄⁻ | nucleic acids, phospholipids, **ATP** |
| S | SO₄²⁻ | constituent of proteins |

|---|---|---|

## Mycorrhizae | 4 min
- **Most vascular plants form symbiosis with fungi**; arbuscular mycorrhiza ~**71 %** of species, non-mycorrhizal ~8 %, orchid ~10 % (Brundrett & Tedersoo 2018) (p. 21)
- Plant–mycorrhizal symbioses are about **475 million years old** (p. 22)
- Hyphae colonise only **young roots** (synchronised development) (p. 23)
- **Ectomycorrhizae (ECM)** (p. 24–27):
- ↳ Roots enclosed by a **fungal sheath (mantle)**; hyphae grow **between** cortex cells → **Hartig net**; fungus up to **40 % of root mass**
- ↳ Roots thicker and repeatedly branched; root hairs replaced by **extramatrical hyphae** in the soil
- ↳ ~**2,000 plant species** with > **5,000 fungal species**; evolved in the **Cretaceous**; mainly **temperate trees** (conifers, deciduous), eucalyptus; important in **forestry**
- ↳ Plant sucrose → fungal trehalose, mannitol; up to **10 %** of tree photosynthate goes to the rhizosphere
- ↳ **Benefits to trees**: more nutrients (especially **phosphate**); hyphae explore more soil; fungal hormones increase active root area; tolerance to **drought, heat, pH extremes, heavy metals**; protection against root pathogens
- ↳ **Benefit to fungus**: **4–20 %** of the plant's fixed carbon
- **Arbuscular mycorrhizae (AM) = endomycorrhizae** (p. 28–29):
- ↳ Most common (~**72 %**); ~**300,000** plant species but only ~**230** fungal species (**Glomeromycota**)
- ↳ Very old: spores as old as the first land plants (**460 million years**)
- ↳ Low specificity but **obligate** for the fungus
- ↳ Hyphae penetrate **into** root cells forming **arbuscules** (tree-like branching, like haustoria; break down after ~**2 weeks**, releasing nutrients) and **vesicles**
- ↳ Mainly in herbaceous communities and the tropics; ecto and endo are not mutually exclusive
- ↳ Signalled by **strigolactones** (Czech–US–Dutch discovery, 2019)
- **Orchid mycorrhizae** (endo) (p. 30): tiny seeds without reserves must be infected soon after germination; the fungus supplies **carbohydrates**; the orchid digests hyphal coils and makes antifungals; fungi mostly **Basidiomycetes** (*Rhizoctonia*…); fully **mycotrophic** non-green species such as ***Neottia nidus-avis*** get **trehalose** from the fungus
- **Ericoid mycorrhizae** (p. 31): family **Ericaceae** (heather, blueberries, cranberries, *Rhododendron*); fungus ***Hymenoscyphus ericae*** (ascomycete); adaptation to **acidic, nutrient-poor soils** (bogs, heaths, boreal forest); fungal coils in epidermal cells of fine hair roots
- **Arbutoid** (p. 32): *Arctostaphylos*, *Arbutus*, Pyrolaceae; extensive intracellular coils
- **Monotropoid** (p. 32): achlorophyllous Monotropaceae (*Monotropa hypopitys*, *M. uniflora*); hyphae **never penetrate cell walls**; carbon comes via boletes from neighbouring conifers

## Bacterial symbiosis & parasitic plants · Stem & vascular system | 3 min
- **Root-nodule symbiosis**: mostly **Fabaceae**; bacteria ***Rhizobium*, *Bradyrhizobium*** fix **atmospheric N₂** into organic compounds (p. 33)
- ↳ Bacteria enter via **root hairs** (hair curls, infection thread) → cortex cells divide → **nodule**; bacteria enclosed by host membrane
- ↳ Plant gives **carbohydrates**; gets amino acids, amides, ureides
- **Parasitic plants**: roots modified into **haustoria** connecting to the host's vascular bundles (p. 34)
- ↳ Semi-parasite: **mistletoe (*Viscum album*)** on *Pinus sylvestris*
- ↳ Parasite: **dodder (*Cuscuta*, Convolvulaceae)**


### Stem & vascular system
- **Xylem**: tracheids and vessels; **primary xylem from procambium**, **secondary from vascular cambium** (p. 35)
- **Phloem**: sieve tubes, companion cells, bast fibres; primary from apical meristem, secondary from vascular cambium (p. 35)
- Vascular system: **(1) delivers resources** (water, minerals, sugars, amino acids), **(2) mechanical support**, and acts as a **long-distance communication system** (hormones, peptides, proteins, RNA) (p. 36)
- In **roots** vascular tissue is in the **centre** (stability); in **stems** near the **edge** (resists squeezing) (p. 40)
- Xylem cells: **vessels** (multicellular, long, lignified, no end walls) and **tracheids** (single, shorter, dead, hollow) (p. 40)
- Root anatomy: epidermis, root hair, cortex, endodermis, pericycle, xylem/phloem. Stem: sclerenchyma, cambium, pith, cortex. Leaf: upper/lower epidermis, palisade/spongy mesophyll, vascular bundle (p. 41)

| | Phloem | Xylem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | **sugars (sucrose), amino acids** from leaves to storage and growing parts (**translocation**) | **water and minerals** from roots up |
| Direction | **bidirectional** (source → sink) | **unidirectional** (upward) |
| Cells | sieve tubes, companion cells, phloem parenchyma, bast fibres | tracheids, vessel elements, xylem parenchyma, sclerenchyma |
| Nature | **living** (little cytoplasm, no nucleus) | **dead** at maturity, hollow, lignified (waterproof, strong) |
| Structure | sieve plates with pores in cross walls | no cross walls → continuous water column; protoxylem / metaxylem |
| Position in bundle | outer side | centre ("star-shaped") |

## Leaf · Transpiration & stomata | 2 min
- **Lamina** = main food-making part; most functions occur in the **mesophyll**; RuBisCO > 50 % of leaf protein (p. 38)
- Leaf tissues: phloem, xylem, sclerenchyma, collenchyma, parenchyma, palisade and spongy mesophyll, bundle-sheath cells (p. 39)


### Transpiration & stomata
- Root hairs absorb water by **osmosis**; xylem moves water and minerals upward against gravity = **transpiration stream** (one-way) (p. 42)
- **Transpiration** = evaporation of water from aerial parts (mostly leaves) → **suction (transpiration pull)** draws water up from the roots; depends on temperature, wind, humidity, stomata number and leaf surface; also **cools** the plant (p. 42)
- **Stoma**: pore between **2 guard cells**; open → CO₂ in, H₂O out; the water-vapour gradient (~100 % inside the leaf) is far steeper than the CO₂ gradient, and water is a smaller molecule, so water is lost faster than CO₂ enters (p. 43)
- **Regulation** (p. 44):
- ↳ By day, chloroplasts make sugar → **lower water potential of guard cells**
- ↳ Guard cells take in water by **osmosis** → **turgid**, "sausage-shaped" → **stoma opens**
- ↳ At night sugar is used up → guard cells lose water → **stoma closes**
- ↳ When roots are damaged and can't absorb water, the plant closes its stomata


## Phloem translocation · Biomechanics, environment, C3/C4/CAM | 2 min
- Living sieve tubes; photosynthetic **glucose → sucrose** in leaves
- **Translocation** = transport of soluble products (sucrose, amino acids) up and down through sieve tubes; **active, using ATP**; loading sucrose raises osmotic pressure → water enters → flow to lower-pressure tissues (e.g. roots → buds); amino acids go to fruits and seeds
- Costs more energy in tall plants


### Biomechanics, environment, C3/C4/CAM
- **Plant biomechanics**; **mechanobiology**: how forces shape development; key challenge = **mechanotransduction** (sensing mechanical signals) (p. 46)
- **Environmental physiology**: responses to water loss, air chemistry, crowding (p. 47)
- **C3** (p. 48): Calvin cycle only; first product **3C PGA**; RuBisCO carboxylation with low yield due to **parallel oxygenation** (an old enzyme from a low-O₂ era); most plants, temperate; drought and heat → stomata closed → O₂ up, CO₂ down → Calvin cycle stops
- **C4** (p. 49): evolved when **CO₂ fell in the Miocene**; **Kranz anatomy** with mesophyll (M) and bundle-sheath (BS) cells; in M, CO₂ fixed by **PEP** (no RuBisCO) = **Hatch–Slack cycle** → **oxaloacetate (4C)** → malate → BS → CO₂ → Calvin cycle **without photorespiration** → high sugar production. Examples: **sugar cane (*Saccharum officinarum*), maize (*Zea mays*)**, sugar beet listed
- **CAM** (p. 50): succulents; **temporal** separation; modified C4; avoids photorespiration in drought and heat


## Pigments · Phytohormones | 6 min
- Include **porphyrins** (chlorophyll), **carotenoids**, **anthocyanins**; cyanobacteria also have **phycoerythrin, phycocyanin, allophycocyanin**. They absorb some wavelengths and reflect others, which gives their colour (p. 51)
- **Chlorophyll**: primary pigment; a **porphyrin** absorbing **red and blue**, reflecting **green**. Land plants and green algae have **chlorophyll a and b**; kelps, diatoms and heterokonts have **c instead of b**; red algae have chlorophyll a (p. 52)
- **Carotenoids**: red/orange/yellow **tetraterpenoids**; **accessory pigments** gathering light chlorophyll misses; carotene (carrots), **lutein** (yellow), **lycopene** (tomatoes); antioxidants, good for eyesight (p. 53)
- **Anthocyanins**: **water-soluble**, red/blue/violet (blackberries, raspberries, blueberries, cherries); absorb harmful radiation, transport monosaccharides, **osmotic adjustment** in drought and cold; antioxidant against stress (p. 54)
- **Betalains**: red or yellow, water-soluble, **indole-derived from tyrosine**; only in **Caryophyllales** (cacti, amaranth, **beetroot**); **never occur together with anthocyanins**; used as food colouring; possibly fungicidal (p. 55)


### Phytohormones
- Signal molecules in **extremely low concentrations**, **less specific than animal hormones**; control everything from embryogenesis to dormancy and germination; affect gene expression, division and growth (p. 56)
- **Unlike animals, plants have no hormone-producing glands**: every cell can make hormones, so they are often not transported (p. 56)
- Fungi and bacteria make similar chemicals; plants also have light-sensitive compounds such as **phytochrome** (p. 56)
- Plants regulate hormone levels: timing, location (meristems), storage, inactivation, breakdown, transport/dilution; hormones regulate each other (p. 57)
- **Six main classes: auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins, abscisic acid (ABA), ethylene, brassinosteroids**. Others: polyamines, **jasmonates, salicylic acid**, phenolics, peptide hormones, **strigolactones** (carotenoid derivatives) (p. 58)

| Hormone | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Auxins** (IAA) | needed for development and **tropisms**; cell enlargement, bud formation, **root initiation**; with cytokinins control growth of stems, roots, fruits. **Kögl 1933** isolated it from **human urine** → **indole-3-acetic acid**; confirmed in plants **1946**; synthetic **NAA** stimulates rooting; auxin transport gives positional information |
| **Cytokinins** (e.g. zeatin) | **cell division**, shoot formation; first isolated from **yeast**; **delay senescence**; mediate auxin transport; internode length, leaf growth; the **auxin : cytokinin ratio** drives major growth phases |
| **Gibberellins** (e.g. GA1) | first from the fungus ***Gibberella fujikuroi*** (abnormal rice growth); strongly promote **cell elongation**; rise as ABA falls (end of dormancy) |
| **Abscisic acid (ABA)** | **growth inhibitor**, survival in harsh environments; conserved in all plants incl. mosses; root growth, **seed drying, storage proteins**; formed in dormant buds, seeds, tubers; induces **dormancy**, leaf fall; accelerates ageing. **Water stress**: ABA moves from roots to leaves, changes K⁺/Na⁺ in guard cells → turgor lost → **stomata close** |
| **Ethylene** | a **gas**, diffuses out of cells; made in fast-growing cells, especially in **darkness**; inhibits leaf expansion; stops elongation and makes stems **swell** (thicker trunks in wind-stressed "flagged" trees; growing around obstacles); **fruit ripening** (climacteric) |
| **Brassinosteroids** | the **only steroid hormones** in plants (polyhydroxysteroids); cell elongation and division, **gravitropism**, stress resistance, xylem differentiation; inhibit root growth and leaf abscission; **brassinolide** from rapeseed pollen, **1979** |
| **Jasmonates** | lipid-based, first from **jasmine oil**; defence against **herbivores and necrotrophic pathogens**; jasmonic acid most active; germination, seed storage proteins, root growth |
| **Salicylic acid** | phenol-related; from **white willow bark (*Salix alba*)**; defence against **biotrophic pathogens**; volatile **methyl-SA / methyl-JA** warn neighbouring plants; abiotic stress responses; precursor of **aspirin** |
| **Strigolactones** | made by **roots**; stimulate germination of root parasites (first found in ***Striga lutea***); promote **AM fungi**; **inhibit shoot branching**; senescence, phosphate starvation, salt tolerance, light signalling |

## Plant movements · Photoperiodism & circadian rhythms | 3 min
- **Physical** (living or dead parts): **hygroscopic** (different swelling of walls, e.g. cones opening when dry); based on water cohesion (p. 66)
- **Vital** (living plants only) (p. 66):
- ↳ **Taxis**: movement of the whole organism by flagella/cilia toward/away from a one-sided factor (unicellular algae)
- ↳ **Tropisms**: **growth** movements caused by a **one-sided** stimulus, only in growing parts; **positive** (toward) or **negative** (away)
- ↳ **Nastic movements**: **not dependent on stimulus direction**; growth-based or **turgor-based** (sleep movements); may be protective
- **Passive**: spores, pollen, seeds carried by wind, water, animals (p. 66)
- **Tropism** in 3 steps: **perception → signal transduction → directional growth**; slow (p. 67)
- ↳ **Phototropism** (toward light), **geotropism/gravitropism** (roots positive, shoots negative), **chemotropism**, **heliotropism** (following the sun), **hygrotropism** (moisture), **thermotropism**
- **Nastic movements**: faster, non-oriented; may or may not involve growth (p. 68)
- ↳ **Epinasty** (downward bending from growth on top), **hyponasty** (upward), **photonasty** (flower opening/closing in light), **nyctinasty** (night), chemonasty, hydronasty, thermonasty, **seismonasty** (shock), geonasty, **thigmonasty/haptonasty** (touch)


### Photoperiodism & circadian rhythms
- **Photoperiodism** = response to the **length of night/dark period**; angiosperms sense it with **phytochrome** or **cryptochrome** as a signal to flower (p. 69)
- **Short-day, long-day and day-neutral** plants (p. 69)
- **Phytochrome**: two forms. **Red light** (day) converts **Pr → Pfr (active)** → growth; **far-red** (shade/dark) converts **Pfr → Pr (inactive)**. Lets the plant tell day from night (p. 69)
- **Cryptochromes** absorb **blue light and UV-A**; entrain the circadian clock (p. 69)
- **Circadian rhythms**: endogenous, entrainable ~**24 h** oscillations, also in prokaryotes; govern leaf movement, growth, germination, stomata, enzyme and photosynthetic activity, scent emission; tell the plant the season and when to flower (p. 70)
- ↳ Cyanobacteria (*Synechococcus*) separate photosynthesis and N₂ fixation in time; gene cluster **kaiABC**
- ↳ **Light** synchronises the clock via phytochromes (red) and cryptochromes (blue); **PhyA** dominates in dark-grown seedlings and degrades in light

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Stomatal opening and closing (p. 44, p. 61)
1. Light → photosynthesis in guard cells → sugars accumulate
2. Water potential drops → water enters by osmosis → guard cells turgid → stoma opens
3. Night (sugar used) or water stress (ABA from roots alters K⁺) → water leaves → stoma closes

### Transpiration stream (p. 42)
Soil water → root hairs (osmosis) → root xylem → stem xylem → leaf → evaporation through stomata → transpiration pull

### Root-nodule formation (p. 33)
Rhizobia gather at a root hair → hair curls → infection thread → cortex cells divide → nodule → bacteria fix N₂; plant supplies sugars

### Tropism (p. 67)
Perception → signal transduction → directional growth

### Phytochrome switch (p. 69)
Red light (day): Pr → Pfr (active, growth/flowering signals); far-red/dark: Pfr → Pr (inactive)
