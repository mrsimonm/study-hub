## Molecular & cell biology review · What are viruses? | 2 min
- DNA vs RNA: DNA double-stranded, deoxyribose, thymine; RNA usually single-stranded, ribose, uracil (p. 4–6)
- RNA structure levels: **sequence → secondary structure (e.g. tRNA cloverleaf) → tertiary structure** (p. 7)
- Central dogma including **reverse transcription** and **RNA replication** (both used by viruses) (p. 8, p. 53)
- Amino acids grouped as **non-polar, polar, positively charged (Lys, Arg, His), negatively charged (Asp, Glu)** (p. 9)
- Protein structure: **primary** (amino-acid chain) → **secondary** (helix) → **tertiary** (folded polypeptide) → **quaternary** (aggregation of 2+ polypeptides) (p. 10)
- **Prokaryotic cell**: pilus, capsule, cell wall, plasma membrane, nucleoid (DNA), cytoplasm, ribosomes, flagellum. **Eukaryotic cell**: nucleus, nucleolus, ER (smooth and rough), Golgi, mitochondria, lysosome, peroxisome, cytoskeleton, ribosomes (p. 11)


### What are viruses?
- "A virus is a piece of bad news wrapped up in protein": **Sir Peter Medawar** (Nobel Prize 1960) (p. 13, p. 42)
- Viruses are **non-cellular, obligate intracellular parasites** (p. 14)
- **"Are viruses alive?" → virocell concept**: an infected cell turned into a virus factory; diagram of a virus (genetic material + protein capsid + surface proteins) vs an enveloped virus (+ envelope) (p. 15)

### History of virology
- **~1500 BC**: Egyptian stele of a priest with a polio-deformed leg (p. 28)
- **Variolation** in ancient China (inoculation with smallpox material) (p. 29)
- **Edward Jenner**: first **vaccination**, **14 May 1796** (James Phipps, cowpox against smallpox) (p. 30)
- **Louis Pasteur**: artificial vaccines; **6 July 1885** vaccinated Joseph Meister (rabies) (p. 31)
- Discovery via **tobacco mosaic virus (TMV)** (p. 32):
- ↳ **1876, Adolf Mayer**: TM is infectious, caused by a small bacterium or a toxin
- ↳ **1892, Dmitri Ivanovsky**: the agent passes through **bacterial filters**
- ↳ **1898, Martinus Beijerinck**: *contagium vivum fluidum* (soluble living germ) = **virus** (Latin "poison")
- ↳ **1898, Friedrich Loeffler**: **foot-and-mouth disease (FMD)** is also caused by a filterable virus (first animal virus)
- Light-microscope resolution is **~200 nm (λ/2)**, so viruses can't be seen; **1931: viruses first observed in the electron microscope** (p. 33) (joke about Jára Cimrman's stacked microscopes)

## Diversity · Virus structure | 2 min
- Cellular life looks diverse but is **not so diverse on the cellular/molecular scale** (only prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells) (p. 35–36)
- Viruses are **very diverse**, also molecularly: **RNA genomes (ds, +ss, −ss, circular)** and **DNA genomes (ds, ss)** (p. 37–38)
- Genetic similarity to humans: human 99.999 %, chimpanzee 98 %, mouse 92 %, fruit fly 44 %, yeast 26 %, tree 18 % (p. 39)
- All cellular life shares **LUCA** (last universal common ancestor, ~3 billion years ago) (p. 40)
- **No gene is universal to all viruses**. HIV diversity within one patient (5 %) ≈ diversity among all primates; dengue virus (DENV) isolates differ by ~30 % ≈ diversity among all vertebrates (p. 41)


### Virus structure
- **HIV structure** (p. 43): phospholipid **envelope** with **envelope glycoproteins** (gp120, gp41); **matrix proteins**; **capsid proteins = capsomers → capsid**; genomic RNA; **capsid + genomic nucleic acid = nucleocapsid**; virus-associated enzymes (reverse transcriptase, integrase, protease)
- Shapes: **icosahedral, filamentous (helical), head-tail** (bacteriophages) (p. 44, p. 50)
- **Icosahedral symmetry**: built from pentamers and hexamers; capsid size described by triangulation number **T** (T = 1, 3, 4, 7…; T = h² + hk + k², e.g. h=4, k=2 → T=28) (p. 45–47)

## Classification · Viral replication: 6 steps | 3 min
- **Unenveloped vs enveloped** (envelope = lipid membrane with surface proteins) (p. 49)
- **By structure**: icosahedral, filamentous, head-tail (p. 50)
- **By host**: bacterial viruses = **bacteriophages**; plant viruses; animal viruses (mammal, avian, insect viruses, **arboviruses**…) (p. 51)
- **By nucleic acid**: dsDNA, ssDNA, dsRNA, +ssRNA, −ssRNA, circular RNA (p. 52)
- **Baltimore classification** (by genome and route to +mRNA) (p. 54):
- ↳ **I dsDNA**: adenovirus, herpes simplex virus
- ↳ **II ssDNA (+)**: parvovirus
- ↳ **III dsRNA**: reovirus, rotavirus
- ↳ **IV +ssRNA**: poliovirus
- ↳ **V −ssRNA**: influenza virus, Ebola virus
- ↳ **VI ssRNA-RT** (+RNA → DNA): retrovirus (HIV)
- ↳ **VII dsDNA-RT**: hepatitis B virus
- **Taxonomy of SARS-CoV-2** (p. 55): Realm *Riboviria* → Kingdom *Orthornavirae* → Phylum *Pisuviricota* → Class *Pisoniviricetes* → Order *Nidovirales* → Family *Coronaviridae* → Subfamily *Coronavirinae* → Genus *Betacoronavirus* → Subgenus *Sarbecovirus* → Species *Severe acute respiratory syndrome–related coronavirus*; virus SARS-CoV-2, strain Omicron BA.1


### Viral replication: 6 steps
1. **Attachment**: binding to a **receptor** on the host cell, sometimes needing **co-receptors** (HIV gp120 → CD4, then co-receptor CCR5/CXCR4 → fusion) (p. 58)
2. **Penetration**: **membrane fusion**, **receptor-mediated endocytosis**, or **membrane penetration** (phage injecting DNA) (p. 59)
3. **Uncoating**: release of the genome from the capsid (can be blocked by host factors, e.g. MxB) (p. 60)
4. **Replication**: biosynthesis of viral nucleic acids and proteins; **RNA viruses usually in the cytoplasm, DNA viruses usually in the nucleus**, with many exceptions (e.g. DNA viruses replicating in the cytoplasm) (p. 61)
5. **Assembly**
6. **Release**: **budding, exocytosis, cell lysis** (p. 62)

## Pathogenesis & ecology · Resources | 2 min
- Pathogenesis cycle (p. 64): virus in environment → portal of entry (implantation) → spread through lymph and blood (primary viraemia) → target organs and secondary viraemia → disease → site of shedding → shedding → spread back to the environment
- **Ecological cycles**: e.g. Ebola, where **bats are believed to be the reservoir hosts**; spillover through other wild animals; human-to-human transmission drives epidemics (p. 66)
- **Hosts and vectors**: sylvatic cycle (non-human primates + mosquitoes *Haemagogus*, *Sabethes*), ecotone, urban cycle (humans + *Aedes aegypti/albopictus*), e.g. yellow fever; vertical transmission among mosquitoes (p. 67)
- **Beneficial viruses?** (p. 68–69)
- ↳ Bacteriophages (phages attacking bacteria); a vaccination cartoon; the **placenta** (p. 68)
- ↳ Three-way symbiosis: **a virus in a fungus in a plant gives thermal tolerance** (Science, 2007; the plant survives 65 °C soil only with the virus-infected fungus) (p. 69)

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Viral replication cycle (p. 57–62)
1. Attachment (receptor ± co-receptor)
2. Penetration (fusion / endocytosis / injection)
3. Uncoating
4. Replication (RNA → cytoplasm, DNA → nucleus, usually)
5. Assembly
6. Release (budding / exocytosis / lysis)

### Discovery of viruses (TMV) (p. 32)
1. 1876 Mayer: infectious
2. 1892 Ivanovsky: filterable
3. 1898 Beijerinck: "virus", contagium vivum fluidum
4. 1898 Loeffler: FMD virus (animal)
5. 1931: electron microscope (p. 33)

### Pathogenesis (p. 64)
Environment → portal of entry → lymph/blood (primary viraemia) → target organs (secondary viraemia) → disease → shedding → environment
