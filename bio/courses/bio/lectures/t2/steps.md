## Central dogma · DNA structure recap · DNA polymerase · DNA replication: 3 steps | 4 min
- DNA → DNA = **replication** (enzyme **DNA polymerase**)
- DNA → RNA = **transcription** (enzyme **RNA polymerase**)
- RNA → protein = **translation** (on the **ribosome**)
- RNA → DNA = reverse ("back") transcription; proteins have building and catalytic (enzyme) functions (p. 1)


### DNA structure recap
- Macromolecule, double helix; each strand is a chain of 4 nucleotide types (deoxyribose + base + phosphate)
- A–T (2 H-bonds), C–G (3 H-bonds); in RNA A–U (2)
- Strands have **directionality**: **5′ end** and **3′ end**


### DNA polymerase
- Family of enzymes carrying out all forms of DNA replication
- Adds nucleotides to the **3′ end** of an existing chain, matched to the template, by forming phosphodiester bonds
- Error rate **< 1 per 10⁷ nucleotides**; some polymerases also **proofread** (remove mismatched bases)


### DNA replication: 3 steps
"For a cell to divide, it must first replicate its DNA!" (p. 4)

**1) Initiation** (p. 4–6)
- Starts at **origins of replication**, recognised by **initiator proteins** → **pre-replication complex** unwinds DNA
- Two **helicases** bind → two **replication forks** (Y-shaped), extending both ways = **replication bubble** (p. 5)
- Eukaryotic chromosomes have **multiple origins** (hundreds to thousands) → simultaneous replication (p. 5)
- **Helicase** (helix-destabilising enzyme): separates the strands at the fork, working behind topoisomerase (p. 6)
- **Topoisomerase**: relaxes supercoiled DNA (p. 6)
- **Single-strand binding (SSB) proteins**: bind ssDNA and prevent re-annealing (p. 6)

**2) Elongation** (p. 4, p. 7–8)
- DNA polymerase **cannot initiate** a new strand; it only extends an existing 3′ end (p. 7)
- **Primase** (a specialised RNA polymerase) makes a short RNA **primer** complementary to the template; then DNA polymerase extends it (p. 7)
- Synthesis only **5′ → 3′**; template strands are **antiparallel**, so the new strands grow in opposite directions (p. 8)
- ↳ **Leading strand**: continuous, toward the fork, **one primer**
- ↳ **Lagging strand**: discontinuous, away from the fork, **many primers** → **Okazaki fragments**, each with its own RNA primer

**3) Termination** (p. 4, p. 9)
- Forks meet at many points (multiple origins)
- Linear chromosomes: replication **can't reach the very end** → ends shorten each division
- **Telomeres** = repetitive DNA at chromosome ends protecting genes from loss; telomere shortening is normal in somatic cells → limited number of divisions
- In the **germ line**, **telomerase** extends telomere repeats
- **2009 Nobel Prize**: discovery of telomere function and telomerase

## Transcription · Post-transcriptional modification | 2 min
- First step of **gene expression**: a DNA segment is copied into RNA (mainly mRNA) by **RNA polymerase**; RNA then serves as the template for protein synthesis (p. 10)
- RNA features: **D-ribose**; function = realisation of genetic information (proteosynthesis); usually no double helix (only tRNA has 3D shape); **U replaces T**; mRNA arises in transcription, rRNA builds ribosomes with proteins (p. 11)
- Steps (p. 13–18):
- ↳ RNA polymerase + **general transcription factors** bind the **promoter**
- ↳ RNA polymerase opens a **transcription bubble** (breaks H-bonds between DNA strands)
- ↳ RNA polymerase adds ribonucleotides complementary to **one DNA strand** (template)
- ↳ The RNA sugar-phosphate backbone forms
- ↳ H-bonds of the RNA–DNA helix break, releasing the new RNA
- ↳ Simplified version on p.14: unwinding → polymerase attachment → mRNA synthesis → helix re-winding
- Base pairing DNA → mRNA: **A→U, T→A, C→G, G→C**. Example: DNA CAT-TCG-TTA-TAG → mRNA **GUA-AGC-AAU-AUC** (p. 15)


### Post-transcriptional modification
- In eukaryotes, the **primary transcript (pre-mRNA)** is converted to mature mRNA in the **nucleus**; mRNA is then transported to the cytoplasm for translation (p. 19)
- Eukaryotic DNA has **exons** (coding) and **introns** (non-coding); the whole gene is transcribed into pre-mRNA (p. 20)
- **RNA splicing**: introns are removed and exons joined into one continuous molecule (p. 20–21)

## Translation | 2 min
- mRNA is decoded in a **ribosome** → polypeptide → folds into an active protein (p. 22)
- **Codon** = triplet of mRNA bases encoding one amino acid; the code table has **64 entries** (p. 23–25)
- Properties of the genetic code (p. 24–25):
- ↳ **Degenerate** (several codons per amino acid) → softens the effect of mutations
- ↳ **Non-overlapping**, read **5′ → 3′**
- ↳ **Universal** (highly similar in all organisms)
- **Start codon: AUG** (methionine); **stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA** (p. 27, p. 30, p. 32)
- 20 amino acids listed with abbreviations (Phe, Leu, Ile, Met, Val, Ser, Pro, Thr, Ala, Tyr, His, Gln, Asn, Lys, Asp, Glu, Cys, Trp, Arg, Gly) (p. 27)
- **tRNA** delivers amino acids; **rRNA** links them; the ribosome pairs tRNA **anticodons** with mRNA **codons** (p. 28)
- Steps (p. 29–32):
- ↳ **Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase** attaches each amino acid to its tRNA using **ATP**
- ↳ **Small ribosomal subunit** binds the **5′ end** of the mRNA
- ↳ It scans to the **start codon AUG**; the first tRNA and the **large subunit** join
- ↳ tRNAs with matching anticodons enter the ribosome
- ↳ The first tRNA releases its amino acid and leaves; the next moves over
- ↳ Amino acids join by **peptide bonds**
- ↳ At a **stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA)** the finished polypeptide is released

## Post-translational modification (PTM) | 2 min
- **PTM** = covalent, usually enzymatic modification of proteins after synthesis; extends the chemistry of the 20 amino acids (p. 33)
- **Phosphorylation** (adds phosphate to Ser, Thr or Tyr) = **most common PTM**, regulates enzyme activity (p. 33–34)
- Others (diagram p.34): hydroxylation, methylation, lipidation, acetylation, glycosylation (sugar to N or O), ubiquitination (tags protein for degradation), SUMOylation, disulfide bonds (between cysteines)

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### DNA replication (p. 4–9)
1. Initiator proteins bind the origin → pre-replication complex
2. Topoisomerase relaxes supercoils; helicase unwinds; SSB proteins hold strands apart
3. Primase lays RNA primers (1 on leading, many on lagging)
4. DNA polymerase extends 5′→3′: leading continuously, lagging as Okazaki fragments
5. Forks meet and terminate; ends shorten (telomeres; telomerase in the germ line)

### Transcription (p. 13–18)
1. RNA polymerase + transcription factors bind the promoter
2. Transcription bubble opens
3. Complementary ribonucleotides are added to the template strand
4. The RNA backbone forms
5. The RNA–DNA helix breaks; RNA is released
6. (Eukaryotes) pre-mRNA → splicing → mRNA → exported to the cytoplasm

### Translation (p. 29–32)
1. Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase charges tRNA (ATP)
2. Small subunit binds the mRNA 5′ end
3. Scans to AUG; first tRNA + large subunit join
4. Matching tRNAs enter; peptide bonds form
5. Empty tRNAs leave; the ribosome moves on
6. Stop codon (UAA/UAG/UGA) → polypeptide released → folding → PTM
