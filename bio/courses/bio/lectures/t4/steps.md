## Cell theory history · Origin of cells | 2 min
- **16th century**: invention of the microscope
- **Robert Hooke, 1665**: first used the word **"cell"** (cork, simple microscope)
- **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek, 1673**: discovered blood cells, spermatozoa, live "animalcules"
- **Matthias Schleiden & Theodor Schwann, 1839**: cells are the **basic unit of life**
- **Rudolf Virchow, ~1850**: living cells arise **only from other living cells** (by division)
- **Principles of cell theory** (p. 3):
- ↳ All organisms are made of one or more cells
- ↳ All life functions occur within cells; cells are the basic units of life
- ↳ All cells come from existing cells
- ↳ Some organisms are unicellular, others multicellular; common features of cells = membrane, nucleus, cell body


### Origin of cells
- **Earth formed 4.6 billion years ago** → **first organic molecules 4.0–4.2 billion** → **first cells 3.8 billion** (p. 4)
- All cells descend from **LUCA** (~3.8 billion years ago) by natural selection; LUCA gave rise to **bacteria and archaea** (p. 5)
- Most organisms are single cells; multicellular organisms arise by division from a single cell; the cell carries all hereditary information (p. 6)


## Prokaryotes vs eukaryotes · Prokaryotic cell | 2 min
- Organ → tissue → cell; the **cell is the structural and functional unit of life** (p. 7)
- The prokaryote/eukaryote distinction is the **most important distinction among organisms**; prokaryotes were the only life for a very long time (p. 8)
- **Prokaryote** (Greek *pro-* before + *karyon* kernel): no nucleus, no membrane-bound organelles, no proper compartmentalisation; mostly unicellular (p. 9)
- **Eukaryote** (*eu-* true): internal membranes + cytoskeleton; nucleus is the characteristic structure (p. 9)


### Prokaryotic cell
- Size **0.1–5 µm**; no compartments; reactions in the cytosol; free ribosomes; **no cytoskeleton**; may have **inclusion bodies** (p. 11)
- Division: **binary fission**; transport: simple and facilitated diffusion (p. 11)
- **Flagella**: movement; **chemotaxis** (movement along a chemical gradient, e.g. toward glucose) (p. 12)
- **3 surface barriers** (p. 12):
- ↳ **Cell membrane**: phospholipid bilayer, **semipermeable**
- ↳ **Cell wall**: usually **peptidoglycan**, permeable
- ↳ **Capsule**: polysaccharides, protection
- **Bacterial chromosome**: circular DNA, **rolling-circle** replication, **haploid**, no introns, transcription and translation in the cytosol (p. 13)
- **Plasmids**: extra-chromosomal circular DNA with additional properties; exchange of plasmids spreads **antibiotic resistance** (p. 13)


## Eukaryotic cell · Ribosomes | 2 min
- Organisms: protozoa, algae, fungi, plants, animals; size **0.5–100 µm** (p. 15)
- Compartmentalised; shape set by the cytoskeleton; **endomembrane system** (ER, Golgi, lysosomes) (p. 15)
- Cell wall only in some: **cellulose** (plants, algae) or **chitin** (fungi, insects, crustaceans, molluscs) (p. 15)
- **Cytoplasm**: in prokaryotes everything inside the plasma membrane; in eukaryotes everything between the plasma membrane and the nuclear envelope; main part = gel-like **cytosol** (p. 16)
- **Nucleus** (p. 17):
- ↳ **Nuclear envelope**: double membrane, continuous with the **ER**; outer layer has ribosomes; inner layer has **lamins** (fibrous proteins; mutations → **laminopathies**)
- ↳ **Nuclear pores**: exchange between nucleus and cytosol
- ↳ **Nucleolus**: **rRNA synthesis**
- ↳ **Nucleoplasm**: gel-like matrix
- **Chromatin**: DNA + proteins + RNA; packages DNA; **euchromatin** (loose) vs **heterochromatin** (highly coiled) (p. 18)
- Eukaryotic DNA: **linear**, multiple rod-shaped chromosomes, packed with **histones**; haploid, diploid or polyploid; transcription in the nucleus, translation in the cytosol (p. 19)


### Ribosomes
- Site of **translation**; large + small subunit; made of ribosomal proteins + rRNA
- Named by **Svedberg units (S)** (sedimentation coefficient):
- ↳ **Eukaryotes: 80S = 40S + 60S**
- ↳ **Prokaryotes: 70S = 30S + 50S**
- Assembled in the nucleus, exported through nuclear pores; free in the cytoplasm or bound to the ER

## Endomembrane organelles · Mitochondria & plastids · Cytoskeleton | 3 min
- **ER**: system of flat vesicles (p. 22)
- ↳ **Rough (granular) ER**: ribosomes; protein synthesis, folding, **glycosylation**
- ↳ **Smooth (agranular) ER**: calcium metabolism, synthesis of **lipids and hormones**
- **Golgi apparatus**: flat **cisternae** + vesicles; completes modification of ER products; sends them via vesicles; structurally and functionally **polarised** (p. 23)
- **Vesicles** (p. 24):
- ↳ **Vacuoles**: mainly plants, storage
- ↳ **Lysosomes**: **hydrolytic enzymes**, intracellular digestion
- ↳ **Phagosomes**: formed by endocytosis; fuse with lysosomes
- ↳ **Peroxisomes**: protect the cell from **hydrogen peroxide**


### Mitochondria & plastids
- **Mitochondria**: "energy centres"; **citrate cycle, β-oxidation of fatty acids**; **double membrane**, inner membrane folded into **cristae**; inner space = **matrix** (p. 25)
- Origin: **an archaeal cell engulfed an aerobic bacterium** (endosymbiosis); **semi-autonomous**, with their own **circular DNA** and protein-synthesis apparatus (p. 26)
- **Plastids** (p. 27–28):
- ↳ **Leukoplasts**: storage
- ↳ **Chromoplasts**: yellow/orange/red pigments (**carotenes, xanthophylls**)
- ↳ **Chloroplasts**: **chlorophyll**; double membrane; **thylakoids** stacked into **grana**; photosynthesis
- ↳ Semi-autonomous; originated as **symbiotic photosynthetic bacteria** taken up by cells that already had mitochondria


### Cytoskeleton
| Filament | Diameter | Protein | Functions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microtubules** | **25 nm** (thickest) | **tubulin** | cell shape, transport, cytoplasmic streaming (cyclosis), **mitotic/meiotic spindle**, centrioles, basal bodies, **cilia and flagella** |
| **Microfilaments** | **5–9 nm** (thinnest) | **actin** (2 intertwined strands) | shape, bear tension, **muscle contraction**, white-blood-cell movement and engulfing |
| **Intermediate filaments** | **8–12 nm** | various fibrous proteins | mechanical strength (skin), anchor nucleus and desmosomes, **nuclear lamina** |
- All three are highly dynamic. Overall functions: shape (esp. in animal cells without a wall), movement, engulfing particles, vesicle transport, chromosome separation, muscle contraction (p. 33)

## Plant vs animal cell · Membranes | 3 min
| Feature | Plant | Animal |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | present | mostly absent |
| Size | large | smaller |
| Plastids | present | absent |
| Vacuole | **large central** | small, many |
| Mitochondria | few | more |
| Lysosomes | almost absent | present |
| Glyoxysomes | present | absent |
- Animal cells also have **centrosome/centrioles**; plant cells have **plasmodesmata** (p. 34, p. 36)
- Appearance changes with age: a meristematic cell has proplastids and small vacuoles; an adult cell has chloroplasts, a large vacuole and intercellular spaces (p. 36)


### Membranes
- Membranes = **lipoprotein complexes**; the plasma membrane is in all living cells (p. 38–39)
- Functions: boundary and traffic control; maintain differences between cytosol and outside; separate organelles; proteins for transport, ion gradients, electrical signals, receptors; take part in metabolism (p. 39)
- **Phospholipid bilayer ~5 nm** thick, held by **non-covalent** bonds, **fluid and dynamic**; hydrophilic phosphate heads outside, hydrophobic fatty-acid tails inside (p. 40)
- Lipids are **amphiphilic** (polar + non-polar parts); hydrophobic molecules cluster to reduce the "ice-cage" ordering of water (p. 41)
- **3 main membrane lipids** (p. 42):
- ↳ **Phospholipids** (most abundant): phosphate head + **2 fatty-acid tails (14–24 C)**; *cis* double bonds make kinks
- ↳ **Glycolipids**: sugars attached, **only on the non-cytosolic (outer) side**
- ↳ **Cholesterol**: sterol, common in eukaryotes
- Bilayer is **asymmetric** (p. 42); lipids self-assemble into **micelles** (cone-shaped) or **bilayers** (cylinder-shaped); bilayers form sealed compartments (p. 43)
- **Membrane proteins**: **~30 % of an animal's proteins**; **transmembrane proteins** (span the bilayer) vs **membrane-associated proteins** (bound via other proteins) (p. 44)

## Membrane transport | 3 min
- The hydrophobic interior is impermeable to polar molecules and **ions** → gradients (energy source); only small hydrophobic molecules pass freely (p. 45–46)
- **Passive transport** (no energy, toward equilibrium, down the gradient) (p. 47, p. 49):
- ↳ **Simple diffusion** through the bilayer
- ↳ **Facilitated diffusion** via specific **channels** or **transporters**
- **Transport proteins** (p. 48):
- ↳ **Transporters (carriers)**: bind the solute, change conformation, release it
- ↳ **Channels**: continuous pores, weak interaction, selective
- **Ion channels**: very fast, **passive only**, ion-selective (Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻), **gated** (open after chemical or electrical stimuli); give muscle and nerve cells electrical excitability; **K⁺ leak channels** maintain the **membrane potential** (p. 50)
- **Active transport** (transporters only): **against the gradient**, needs energy from **(1) another gradient, (2) ATP hydrolysis, (3) light**; like an enzyme reaction but without changing the substrate (p. 51)
- **Gradient-driven (coupled) transport**: e.g. **Na⁺–glucose symport** in intestinal and kidney epithelium; the Na⁺ gradient is kept by the **Na⁺/K⁺ pump** (p. 52)
- **Transcellular glucose transport** (intestine): apical Na⁺–glucose **symporter** brings glucose in → basal glucose **uniporter** lets it out passively → **Na⁺/K⁺ pump** keeps internal Na⁺ low (p. 53)
- **ATP-driven pumps** (p. 54):
- ↳ **P-type**: ion pumps for Na⁺, K⁺, H⁺, Ca²⁺ gradients (e.g. **Na⁺/K⁺ pump**)
- ↳ **ABC transporters** (ATP-binding cassette): pump small molecules
- ↳ **V-type**: pump H⁺ into organelles
- ↳ **F-type ATPase**: reverse of the V-type; in mitochondria; **makes most of the cell's ATP**
- **Na⁺/K⁺ pump**: per ATP → ADP, pumps **3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in** (p. 55)

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Transcellular glucose transport (p. 53)
1. Na⁺–glucose symporter (apical) uses the Na⁺ gradient to pull glucose in
2. Glucose leaves through a uniporter (basolateral), down its gradient
3. The Na⁺/K⁺ pump (ATP) pumps Na⁺ out and keeps the gradient

### Na⁺/K⁺ pump (p. 55)
1. Binds 3 Na⁺ inside
2. ATP → ADP + P (pump phosphorylated) → conformation change → 3 Na⁺ out
3. Binds 2 K⁺ outside → dephosphorylation → 2 K⁺ in

### Endosymbiosis (p. 26, p. 28)
1. An anaerobic archaeal cell engulfs an aerobic bacterium → mitochondrion (double membrane)
2. A cell that already has mitochondria engulfs a photosynthetic bacterium → chloroplast


## Compare and contrast | 2 min
| Feature | Prokaryote | Eukaryote |
|---|---|---|
| Size | 0.1–5 µm | up to 40 µm (0.5–100 µm) |
| Genetic material | circular DNA free in cytosol (nucleoid) | linear chromosomes in a double-membrane nucleus |
| Replication origins | single | multiple |
| Introns | none | present |
| Organelles | none membrane-bound | many |
| Cell wall | present, **peptidoglycan (murein)** | plants (cellulose), fungi (chitin); never murein; absent in animals |
| Ribosomes | **70S** | **80S** |
| Transcription/translation | together in cytosol | nucleus / cytosol |
| Division | binary fission | mitosis/meiosis |
