## From cells to a body · Unitary vs modular organisms | 2 min
- A cell can be a **fully self-sufficient unit**; many organisms are **unicellular** (e.g. *Paramecium*, *Ceratium*, *Vorticella*) (p. 3, p. 5)
- Multicellular: plants, some algae (seaweeds), fungi (mushrooms), animals (p. 4)
- An "animal" in the correct sense (in the adult stage) is **always multicellular** (e.g. rotifers up to the blue whale) (p. 5–6)
- Cells in a body are **never all the same**: they differ in shape and function (neurons, red blood cells, bone cells…) (p. 7)
- **Hierarchy**: **cells → tissues** (groups of cells with similar function) **→ organs** (stomach, heart, kidney, lung) **→ organ systems** (digestive, circulatory, excretory, respiratory) **→ body** (p. 8)
- **Four types of tissue** (p. 9–13):
- ↳ **Epithelial**: covering/lining layers, e.g. simple squamous (lungs), simple cuboidal (kidney tubules), simple columnar (intestine), stratified squamous (oesophagus), pseudostratified ciliated columnar (nasal passage), transitional (urinary system) (p. 10)
- ↳ **Connective**: ligaments, tendons (fibrous), loose connective tissue, **adipose** tissue, **cartilage**, **bone**, **blood** (body fluids) (p. 11)
- ↳ **Muscle**: movement. **Skeletal, smooth, cardiac** (p. 12)
- ↳ **Nervous**: information transfer. Neurons can be **unipolar, bipolar, pseudounipolar, multipolar** (p. 13)


### Unitary vs modular organisms
- **Unitary**: the body is clearly defined (snail, beetle, fish, mammal, zebra)
- **Modular**: body made of **modules** that are fully or partly autonomous and form a **colony**, sometimes linked by organ systems (**sponges, corals**)

## Formation of a new body | 2 min
- **A) From part of a parent body (asexual)**: modular organisms split off modules; animals with high **regeneration** ability (e.g. *Hydra*, planarian flatworms regrowing heads) (p. 16)
- **B) From one cell**, usually a **fertilised egg**; the first stages are always the same (p. 17):
- ↳ **fertilised egg (zygote)** → 2. **morula** → 3. **blastula** → 4. **gastrula**
- Cell differentiation is visible very early: **3 germ layers: ectoderm, endoderm, mesoderm** (gastrulation; blastopore, archenteron; later neural tube, notochord, somites, coelom) (p. 18)
- ***Hydra*** stays at the **gastrula level** with only **two cell layers** (epidermis, gastrodermis, mesoglea between; gastrovascular cavity; one opening = mouth) (p. 19)
- **Jellyfish**: more layers but derived from only **two germ layers (ectoderm, endoderm)**; mouth/anus is one opening (p. 20)
- Body cavities (p. 21–23):
- ↳ **Flatworms (planarian) = acoelomate**: no body cavity; mesoderm fills the space (mesenchyme)
- ↳ **Roundworms = pseudocoelomate**: cavity (**pseudocoel**) between gut (endoderm) and muscle (mesoderm)
- ↳ **Annelids (earthworm) = coelomate**: true **coelom** lined by mesoderm (peritoneum) on both sides
- **Symmetry** (p. 24): **radial** (sea anemone, several planes) vs **bilateral** (crayfish: anterior/posterior, dorsal/ventral, one plane)

## Body functions · Respiration systems | 3 min
- The biochemistry is the same as at cell level; the difference is the **spatial distribution** of processes into **organ systems** (p. 25)
- **Main functions** (p. 26–27):
- ↳ **Digestion**: food intake, processing, uptake, delivery
- ↳ **Respiration**: gas exchange (O₂ in, CO₂ out)
- ↳ **Circulation**: transport of substances
- ↳ **Excretion**: collecting and removing unneeded or harmful substances
- ↳ **Reproduction**: new individuals; **not needed for the individual's survival, but for the population's**
- ↳ **Information transfer**: gathering, processing and delivering information; integrates all other functions
- ↳ **Movement** (locomotion; muscles), **support and cohesion** (skeleton), **body border and communication with the environment** (skin), special functions (glands)
- **Important note**: every tissue in every system needs all functions (to respire, be nourished, innervated, repaired…); a system's "intrinsic" function serves the **body as a whole** (p. 27)
- The lecture compares how organ systems are built in different animals, not their biochemistry (p. 28)


### Respiration systems
- **No definite respiratory system**: **planarians and earthworms** exchange gases **through the epidermis** (p. 30)
- **Gills** (aquatic):
- ↳ Sea annelid ***Alitta virens***: **parapodia** act as external gills (p. 31)
- ↳ **Water flea**: gills on the legs (p. 32)
- ↳ **Crayfish**: gills (p. 33)
- ↳ **Mussels**: gills (p. 34–35)
- ↳ **Nudibranch snail**: external gills (p. 36)
- ↳ **Cephalopods**: very well-developed, "perfect" gills (p. 37)
- ↳ **Fish gills**: gill arches with filaments; water flows over them, blood flows in the opposite direction (oxygenated/deoxygenated) (p. 38)
- **Vertebrate lungs**: increasing internal surface from **salamander → lizard → primate**; **birds (pigeon) have lungs + air sacs** (p. 39); dog respiratory tract: nasal cavity, larynx, trachea, lung lobes, diaphragm (p. 29)
- **Insects: tracheal system**: air tubes (**tracheae and tracheoles**) throughout the body, opening to the outside through **spiracles**; also **air sacs** (p. 40–41)
- **Aquatic insect larvae**: **tracheal gills** (e.g. mayfly larvae) (p. 42–43)
- **Chironomid larvae** (bloodworms) contain **haemoglobin**, unusual in insects (p. 44)

## Circulation systems | 3 min
- Body fluid is **haemolymph** (open system) or **blood + lymph** (closed system) (p. 46)
- **Open system**: the heart pumps haemolymph into **sinuses** around organs; returns through **ostia** (p. 46–47)
- ↳ **Insects** (tubular dorsal heart with ostia) (p. 47); **crustaceans** (crayfish, water flea) (p. 48); **mussels** (p. 49)
- **Closed system**: blood stays in vessels, with small branch vessels in each organ (p. 46, p. 50)
- ↳ **Annelids** (earthworm: dorsal vessel = main heart, auxiliary hearts, ventral vessel), **cephalopods**, **vertebrates** (p. 46, p. 50)
- **Capillary bed** (p. 51): artery → arteriole (**precapillary sphincters**) → **capillary** → venule → vein; fluid is **squeezed out by blood pressure** at the arterial end and **re-enters by osmotic attraction** at the venous end
- **Vertebrate hearts** (p. 52–56):

| Group | Loop | Heart | Blood mixing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fish** | **single** loop (heart → gills → body) | **1 atrium + 1 ventricle** | – |
| **Lungfish** | gills + lung | atria + ventricle (partly divided) | some mixing |
| **Amphibians** | double | **2 atria + 1 ventricle** | **mixed** in the ventricle |
| **Most reptiles** | double | 2 atria + 1 ventricle **with partial septum**; right and left aortic arches | partly mixed |
| **Crocodilians, birds, mammals** | **double** (pulmonary + systemic) | **2 atria + 2 ventricles completely divided** | **no mixing** |

- **Blood cells** (p. 57): **erythrocytes** (red), leukocytes (**monocyte, lymphocyte, neutrophil, eosinophil, basophil**), **platelets**
- **Red blood cells**: biconcave discs (p. 58)
- **Haemoglobin** (p. 59): protein of **2 α + 2 β polypeptide chains**, each with a **heme** group (**Fe-protoporphyrin IX**) containing **iron (Fe)**, which binds O₂

## Nervous systems · Digestive systems | 4 min
- Vertebrate (bear): **brain, spinal cord**, cranial nerves, peripheral nerves (radial, ulnar, sciatic, tibial, intercostal) (p. 60)
- **Neuron**: **dendrites** → **cell body** (nucleus) → **axon**; impulses travel from dendrites to the axon end (p. 61)
- **Evolution of nervous systems** (p. 62):
- ↳ **Cnidarian** (Hydra): **nerve net**
- ↳ **Flatworm**: simple brain, **nerve cords**, associative neurons
- ↳ **Earthworm (annelid)**: central nervous system + peripheral nerves (ventral cord)
- ↳ **Mollusc (squid)**: brain, **giant axon**
- ↳ **Arthropod (insect)**: brain, **ventral nerve cord** with ganglia
- ↳ **Echinoderm (sea star)**: **radial nerves**, nerve ring


### Digestive systems
- Food intake and processing (transformation, **absorption**); **digestive cavity** or **digestive tract**; glands producing **enzymes** (p. 63)
- **Gastrovascular (digestive) cavity**: **one opening = mouth and anus**. Cnidarians (*Hydra*) and flatworms (*Planaria*, branched cavity + pharynx) (p. 64)
- **Insects**: **foregut → midgut → hindgut** → anus; **Malpighian tubules** (excretory) join at the midgut/hindgut boundary, so the digestive system includes **parts of the excretory system** (p. 65)
- **Crayfish**: **stomach, hepatopancreas (gland), intestine (gut)** (p. 66)
- **Spider**: mouth, pharynx, stomach (sucking), **digestive diverticula** (glands), midgut, digestive glands; also Malpighian tubules (p. 67)
- **Advanced tract** (molluscs, vertebrates): mouth → gullet → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → anus, with **liver** (p. 68)
- **Molluscs**: bivalves have stomach with gastric shield, **crystalline style**, digestive gland, intestine (snail, squid, mussel) (p. 69)
- **Vertebrate tracts** (shark, salamander, lizard, pigeon, cow): oesophagus, stomach, intestine, liver, gall bladder, pancreas; **cloaca** (non-mammals), **crop** and **gizzard** (birds), **caecum**, rectum (p. 70)
- **Caecum** = a side section of the gut; **trout** have **pyloric caeca**, many "intestines" around the stomach for **food absorption** (p. 71)
- **Hen**: beak, oesophagus, **crop**, **proventriculus**, **gizzard (ventriculus)**, duodenum, small intestine, **two caeca**, large intestine, **cloaca**; liver, gall bladder, pancreas, spleen (p. 72)
- **Liver** = "the **biochemical factory** of the body"; the **gall bladder** under the liver **stores and concentrates bile**, produced by the liver (p. 73)
- **Ruminants: 4-part stomach**: **(1) rumen, (2) reticulum, (3) omasum, (4) abomasum** (p. 74)
- **Symbiotic microorganisms** (p. 75): human gut, ***Escherichia coli***; **ruminants and termites**, microorganisms that **digest cellulose**

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Early development (p. 17–18)
Fertilised egg → cleavage → **morula** → **blastula** → gastrulation → **gastrula** (ectoderm + endoderm, then mesoderm) → neurulation, organs

### Capillary exchange (p. 51)
Artery → arteriole → (precapillary sphincter) → capillary: fluid pushed out by blood pressure; fluid drawn back by osmosis → venule → vein

### Insect digestion (p. 65)
Mouth → foregut → midgut (Malpighian tubules join) → hindgut → anus

### Ruminant digestion (p. 74–75)
Rumen (microbial cellulose digestion) → reticulum → omasum → abomasum (true stomach)
