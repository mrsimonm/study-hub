## Metabolism · Oxidation & reduction | 2 min
- **Catabolism**: breaks complex molecules into simple ones; captures energy in usable form; provides material for biosynthesis (s. 2)
- **Anabolism**: uses energy and simple molecules to **build** molecules and macromolecules; energy also powers enzymes, motor proteins, carrier proteins (s. 2)
- Metabolism diagram: nutrients → catabolism → ATP + smaller molecules → anabolism → larger molecules; heat and wastes released (p. 3–4)
- **Food** = mixture of molecules whose **chemical bonds** provide energy (s. 5)
- Cells get energy by **gradual, stepwise, enzyme-catalysed oxidation** of organic molecules, so useful energy can be harvested (s. 6)


### Oxidation & reduction
- Oxidation = addition of oxygen, or more generally **removal of electrons** (e.g. **Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺**) (s. 7)
- Electrons are conserved, so **oxidation always occurs together with reduction** (redox) (s. 7)
- The terms apply even to **partial electron shifts** in covalent bonds (s. 8)
- Picking up e⁻ usually also picks up H⁺ → adds a hydrogen atom: **A + e⁻ + H⁺ → AH** (s. 9)
- ↳ **Hydrogenation = reduction**; **dehydrogenation = oxidation**
- In organic molecules: **reduction increases the number of C–H bonds; oxidation decreases it** (methane → methanol → formaldehyde → formic acid → CO₂) (s. 10)

## Activated carriers | 3 min
- **Activated carriers**: temporarily store energy from food oxidation in energy-rich covalent bonds; diffuse quickly from energy-generating to biosynthetic sites; store energy as a transferable chemical group or as high-energy electrons; supply both energy and chemical groups. Main ones: **ATP, NADH, NADPH** (s. 11)
- Their formation is **coupled to an energetically favourable reaction** (s. 12)
- **ATP (adenosine triphosphate)** (s. 13–16):
- ↳ Synthesis from ADP is **unfavourable**; hydrolysis to ADP is **favourable** (s. 13)
- ↳ ATP hydrolysis is **coupled** to unfavourable reactions, often by transferring its **terminal phosphate** to another molecule (s. 14)
- ↳ **Condensation**: A–H + B–OH → A–B + H₂O happens in two steps. (1) B–OH + ATP → B–O–PO₃ (**high-energy intermediate**) + ADP; (2) A–H + B–O–PO₃ → A–B + Pi. Net: **A–H + B–OH + ATP → A–B + ADP + Pi + H₂O** (s. 15)
- ↳ Example: **synthesis of glutamine** from glutamic acid + NH₃ (activation by ATP → high-energy phosphorylated intermediate → condensation with ammonia) (s. 16, p. 16)
- **Electron carriers NAD⁺ and NADP⁺** (s. 17–19):
- ↳ Carry "high-energy" electrons and hydrogen in redox reactions
- ↳ Reduced to **NADH / NADPH** by accepting **2 e⁻ + H⁺ (= hydride ion H⁻)** on the **nicotinamide ring**
- ↳ **NADP⁺ has an extra phosphate group**, which does not change electron-transfer properties but changes the **shape**, so the two bind different enzymes (s. 18–19)
- ↳ **NADPH → anabolism**; **NADH → catabolism** (s. 19)

## Glycolysis · Fermentation vs respiration | 2 min
- **Central ATP-producing pathway**; **anaerobic** (no O₂ needed); common to **all organisms**; in the **cytosol** (s. 20)
- Oxidation uses **NAD⁺**; **1 glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvate (3C)** (s. 20)
- **Uses 2 ATP** in early steps, **yields 4 ATP** in final steps → **net 2 ATP + 2 NADH per glucose** (s. 21)
- Detailed energy-generating steps 6–10 (glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate → 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (+NADH) → 3-phosphoglycerate (+ATP) → 2-phosphoglycerate → phosphoenolpyruvate → pyruvate (+ATP)) (p. 22–23)


### Fermentation vs respiration
- For most animal and plant cells, glycolysis is only a **prelude** to the final breakdown (s. 25)
- ↳ **Without O₂ → fermentation**; **with O₂ → respiration** (s. 25)
- **Fermentation** (s. 26–28):
- ↳ Pyruvate and NADH stay in the **cytosol**; pyruvate is converted and excreted (**ethanol + CO₂**, or **lactate**)
- ↳ **Glycolysis is the main ATP source**; NADH gives up its electrons → **NAD⁺ regenerated** for further glycolysis
- ↳ In **anaerobic organisms** (yeasts, bacteria) and in some cells of aerobic organisms when O₂ is short (**skeletal muscle**)
- ↳ **Lactate fermentation**: pyruvate → lactate. **Alcoholic fermentation**: pyruvate → acetaldehyde + CO₂ → ethanol (p. 26–27)
- **Respiration** (s. 29): needs **O₂**; pyruvate enters the **mitochondrion** → **CO₂ + acetyl-CoA**; the acetyl group is completely oxidised by O₂ to **CO₂ + H₂O**; uses a transmembrane ETC and an **H⁺ gradient** to make ATP; proteins and fats are oxidised too

## Mitochondrion · Four basic steps of respiration · Citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) · Electron-transport chain & oxidative phosphorylation | 5 min
- Outer and inner membrane, intermembrane space, **cristae**, **matrix**, ribosomes, granules, **DNA**, **ATP synthase** particles (p. 29)
- **Inner boundary membrane**: protein-import machinery, insertion of new membrane, assembly of respiratory complexes. **Crista membranes**: **ATP synthase** + **respiratory chain** (s. 32)


### Four basic steps of respiration
1. **Glycolysis**
2. **Citric acid cycle**
3. **Electron-transport chain**
4. **Oxidative phosphorylation**


### Citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle)
- Takes place in the **mitochondrial matrix** (s. 33)
- Main products: **CO₂** and high-energy electrons as **NADH and FADH₂**, which go to the ETC (s. 33)
- Needs O₂ indirectly: there is no other efficient way to regenerate NAD⁺ from NADH (s. 33)
- **Oxidative phosphorylation is the only step of oxidative catabolism that uses O₂ directly** (s. 33)
- **Acetyl (2C)** from acetyl-CoA + **oxaloacetate (4C) → citric acid (6C)** → gradual oxidation (6C → 5C → 4C…) → **oxaloacetate regenerated** (s. 34)
- **Net per turn: 3 NADH, 1 GTP, 1 FADH₂, 2 CO₂** (8 steps) (p. 33)


### Electron-transport chain & oxidative phosphorylation
- **Most chemical energy is released here** (s. 35)
- NADH and FADH₂ pass electrons to the ETC in the **inner mitochondrial membrane**; electrons fall to lower energy states; the released energy **pumps H⁺ from the matrix to the intermembrane space** → **H⁺ gradient** → fuels ATP synthesis (s. 35)
- Principle: carriers A → B → C pass a high-energy electron to lower energy; carrier B takes up H⁺ on one side and releases it on the other → H⁺ gradient (s. 36)
- **Components** (s. 37–39):
- ↳ **NADH dehydrogenase complex (Complex I)**: accepts electrons from NADH; passes them via **flavin mononucleotide (FMN)** and **8 iron–sulfur clusters** to **ubiquinone** (lipid-soluble carrier, Q); reduced **ubiquinol** passes them to cytochrome c reductase
- ↳ (diagram) **Succinate dehydrogenase** (from the citric acid cycle) also feeds electrons to ubiquinone (p. 36)
- ↳ **Cytochrome c reductase (cytochrome b-c1 complex)**: a **dimer**, each monomer with **3 cytochrome hemes + 1 Fe–S cluster**; takes electrons from ubiquinol and gives them to **cytochrome c** (small soluble protein in the **crista space**, carries **one electron at a time**)
- ↳ **Cytochrome c oxidase complex**: **2 cytochrome hemes + 3 copper atoms**; takes electrons one at a time from cytochrome c and passes them to **O₂**
- **4 electrons + 4 protons convert 1 O₂ into 2 H₂O** (s. 39)
- Final step: electrons go to **O₂** that has diffused into the mitochondrion, combining with **H⁺ → H₂O** (s. 40)
- **Complete oxidation of 1 glucose → ~30 ATP**; **glycolysis alone → 2 ATP** (s. 41)
- Overview: pyruvate + NADH from glycolysis → acetyl-CoA → citric acid cycle (CO₂) → NADH → oxidative phosphorylation (O₂ → H₂O; ADP + Pi → ATP) (p. 39)
- **Other foods** (proteins → amino acids; carbohydrates → sugars; fats → glycerol + fatty acids) enter the **same pathways** (glycolysis, acetyl-CoA, citric acid cycle) (s. 42)

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Glycolysis (s. 20–21)
Glucose (6C) → [investment: −2 ATP] → 2 × glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate → [payoff: +4 ATP, +2 NADH] → 2 pyruvate (3C). Net: **2 ATP, 2 NADH**

### Fate of pyruvate (s. 25–29)
- No O₂ → fermentation: pyruvate → lactate (muscle) or → acetaldehyde + CO₂ → ethanol (yeast); NAD⁺ regenerated
- O₂ → mitochondrion: pyruvate → acetyl-CoA + CO₂ → citric acid cycle

### Citric acid cycle (s. 34, p. 33)
Acetyl-CoA (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) → citrate (6C) → … (5C, 4C; release 2 CO₂, 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP) → oxaloacetate

### Electron-transport chain (s. 37–40)
NADH → **Complex I** (FMN, 8 Fe–S) → **ubiquinone/ubiquinol** → **cytochrome b-c1** → **cytochrome c** → **cytochrome c oxidase** (2 hemes, 3 Cu) → **O₂ + 4 H⁺ → 2 H₂O**; H⁺ pumped at complexes I, III and IV → ATP synthase
