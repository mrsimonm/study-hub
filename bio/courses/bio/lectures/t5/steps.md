## Membrane organelles & their evolution · Endoplasmic reticulum | 2 min
- Membrane-enclosed organelles: **ER, Golgi, endosomes, lysosomes, peroxisomes, mitochondria, chloroplasts**; their number and shape depend on cell needs and specialisation (p. 2)
- **Evolution of compartments** (p. 3): the ancestor was an **anaerobic archaeon without internal membranes** → the plasma membrane **invaginated** to protect DNA → a **double-membrane nuclear envelope** formed → uptake of an aerobic bacterium (mitochondrion) supplied energy for more membranes
- **Endosymbiont hypothesis** (p. 4): a young eukaryotic cell phagocytosed an oxygen-breathing bacterium and a photosynthetic bacterium without digesting them → symbiosis → integrated into metabolism → **parts of their genomes moved to the nucleus** ("trapped")
- **Evidence** (p. 5):
- ↳ Organelles are **not made de novo**, only by division
- ↳ They have **their own ribosomes** (and DNA)
- ↳ **Two membranes**: inner like bacterial plasma membrane, outer different
- ↳ Prokaryote-like metabolism, protein and rRNA structure, and division
- ↳ **Present-day endosymbioses**: *Paramecium* + *Chlorella* alga; *Hydra* + *Zoochlorella*


### Endoplasmic reticulum
- **Rough ER**: ribosomes on its surface → protein synthesis; **protein sorting**; basic modification (**N-glycosylation**) (p. 6)
- **Smooth ER**: no ribosomes; synthesis of **almost all lipids** (phospholipids, cholesterol); lipid and protein transport to Golgi; **detoxification**; regulation of **cytosolic Ca²⁺** (in muscle: Ca²⁺ release/uptake → contraction/relaxation) (p. 7)

## Protein transport | 4 min
- Synthesis starts on ribosomes in the cytosol; the protein's destination is set by a **sorting signal** in its amino-acid sequence (usually at the start) → nucleus, ER, mitochondria, plastids, peroxisomes or outside (p. 8)
- Proteins for the ER are made by ER-bound ribosomes (**co-translational import**); proteins for the nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts and peroxisomes are finished in the cytosol, then imported (**post-translational import**) (p. 16)
- **Three types of transport** (p. 9):
- ↳ **Gated transport**: cytosol ↔ nucleus through **nuclear pores**
- ↳ **Transmembrane transport**: via **protein translocators**, cytosol → ER lumen or mitochondria
- ↳ **Vesicular transport**: e.g. ER → Golgi in vesicles

#### Nucleus ↔ cytosol (p. 10–11)
- **Inner nuclear membrane**: proteins binding chromosomes + **nuclear lamina** (supporting meshwork); **outer membrane**: continuous with the ER, studded with ribosomes (p. 10)
- **Import**: histones, DNA and RNA polymerases, transcription regulators, RNA-processing proteins. **Export**: almost all RNAs (mRNA, rRNA, tRNA, miRNA, snRNA) (p. 10)
- **Nuclear pore complexes (NPCs)**: ~**30 different proteins = nucleoporins**; small molecules diffuse passively, macromolecules are actively transported; **nuclear localisation signal** → import; **nuclear export signal** → export (p. 11)

#### Into mitochondria & chloroplasts (p. 13)
- They make only a small share of their proteins and **import most from the cytosol**
- Proteins cross both membranes **unfolded**, simultaneously, into the matrix/stroma
- Driven by **ATP hydrolysis + membrane potential** (mitochondria) and **GTP + ATP hydrolysis** (chloroplasts)
- Need a **signal sequence** (N-terminal and cleaved, or internal and retained)

#### Into the ER: signal hypothesis (p. 14–15)
- The ER makes **almost all cell lipids**; a major share of protein synthesis happens on the rough ER (p. 14)
- Only proteins with an **ER signal sequence** enter; it is recognised by the **signal-recognition particle (SRP)**, which binds the growing chain + ribosome and docks on an **SRP receptor** on the ER → translocation through a **protein translocator** channel (p. 14)
- **Signal peptidase** cuts off the signal sequence; the mature protein is released into the ER lumen; the translocator stays closed until a ribosome binds, keeping the barrier intact (p. 15)

## Vesicular transport | 2 min
- **Exocytosis**: secretory pathway delivering molecules to the plasma membrane or outside in **secretory vesicles** (p. 19)
- **Endocytosis**: outside material taken into vesicles → recycled or degraded by lysosomes; brings nutrients (vitamins, cholesterol, iron); **pinocytosis** = uptake of liquids (p. 19)
- Vesicles **bud from one membrane and fuse with another**; transport is selective in content and destination (p. 20)
- **2013 Nobel Prize** (Physiology or Medicine) for vesicle traffic: **Randy Schekman, James Rothman, Thomas Südhof** (p. 21)
- **Three pathways** (p. 22):
- ↳ **Secretory**: ER → Golgi → cell surface
- ↳ **Endocytic**: plasma membrane → endosomes → lysosomes
- ↳ **Retrieval**: returns membrane lipids and proteins to their correct place
- ↳ Destination is set by **molecular markers** (sorting signals)
- **Coat proteins** (p. 23): select cargo and bend membranes into vesicles
- ↳ **COP II**: **ER → Golgi** export of new proteins
- ↳ **COP I**: **retrograde, Golgi (cis) → ER** recycling; also intra-Golgi traffic
- Vesicular tubular clusters move along **microtubules** from ER to Golgi; COP I vesicles return to the ER (p. 26)
- **Trans-Golgi network** sorts: secretory proteins → secretory vesicles; lysosomal proteins → special vesicles (p. 27)

## Golgi apparatus · Lysosomes · Peroxisomes | 2 min
- Stack of disc-like **cisternae**: **cis face (entry)**, **medial** cisternae, **trans face (exit)**
- Receives proteins and lipids from the ER at the cis face → covalent modification (sugars added) in medial cisternae → sorting, packing, labelling → exit at the trans face
- **Main site of carbohydrate synthesis and modification**, incl. **pectins and hemicellulose** (plant walls) and **glycosaminoglycans** (animal extracellular matrix)


### Lysosomes
- Membrane organelles with **~40 types of hydrolytic enzymes** (proteases, nucleases, glycosidases, lipases, phospholipases, phosphatases, sulfatases)
- **Acidic pH 4.5–5**, maintained by an **H⁺ pump**
- Digest macromolecules, debris, phagocytosed microbes; produce nutrients; their material arrives via endosomes
- Material reaches lysosomes by **endocytosis** (macromolecules), **phagocytosis** (large particles, microbes), **pinocytosis** (liquids) and **autophagy** (worn-out organelles, cytosol) (p. 30)


### Peroxisomes
- Small, **single membrane**, **no DNA** (proteins encoded in the nucleus)
- **Oxidative enzymes**: **catalase, urate oxidase**; major sites of O₂ use; may have lowered O₂ in primitive anaerobic cells (O₂ was toxic)
- Detoxification in **liver and kidney**; produce **H₂O₂**, which catalase uses to oxidise alcohol, formaldehyde, formic acid; **~25 % of ethanol** is oxidised to acetaldehyde this way
- In plants: **photorespiration** in leaves and **conversion of lipids to sugars** in germinating seeds


## Energy & chemiosmotic coupling · Mitochondrion structure | 3 min
- **2nd law of thermodynamics**: systems tend toward greater **entropy**; organisms use energy (food, photons) to create order, so they need lots of **ATP** (p. 32)
- ATP comes from two energy-converting organelles of **prokaryotic origin** (p. 33):
- ↳ **Mitochondria**: burn nutrients → ATP by **oxidative phosphorylation**
- ↳ **Chloroplasts**: solar energy → ATP by **photosynthesis**
- **Chemiosmotic coupling** = ATP synthesis driven by an **H⁺ gradient** (p. 34–35):
- ↳ **Stage 1**: high-energy electrons (from oxidised food or light-excited pigments) pass through membrane protein complexes; the released energy **pumps H⁺** → electrochemical gradient
- ↳ **Stage 2**: H⁺ flows back through **ATP synthase** (a proton-driven turbine): **ADP + Pi → ATP**
- **Mitochondrion** (p. 36): citric acid cycle → **NADH** → electrons flow down the electron-transport chain → finally combine with **O₂ → water**
- **Chloroplast** (p. 37): electrons extracted from **water** in **photosystem II** → complexes pump H⁺ → **photosystem I** re-energises them with light → reduce **NADP⁺ → NADPH**


### Mitochondrion structure
- Up to **20 % of cytoplasm volume**; hundreds per active cell; positioned by **microtubules** at sites of high energy demand (p. 38–39)
- ATP with mitochondria is **~15× more efficient** than without (glucose → pyruvate only) (p. 38)
- **Outer membrane** (more permeable; O₂, CO₂ pass quickly) and **inner membrane** folded into **cristae** (electron-transport chain + **ATP synthase**) (p. 41–43)
- **Intermembrane space 20–30 nm**; **crista junctions** (p. 41)
- Cristae greatly enlarge the area: total human cristae area ≈ **a football field** (p. 42)
- **Matrix**: mtDNA in **nucleoids**, ribosomes, **citric acid cycle** (p. 42–43)
- H⁺ gradient on the outer side of the inner membrane; ATP made on the matrix side (p. 43)
- **Consequences of H⁺ pumping** (p. 44):
- ↳ **pH gradient**: matrix ~**pH 8**, intermembrane space lower
- ↳ **Voltage gradient (membrane potential)**: matrix negative, crista space positive
- ↳ Together = **electrochemical gradient** (mV) → **proton-motive force** pulling H⁺ back into the matrix through ATP synthase

## Chloroplast structure | 2 min
- **Outer membrane** highly permeable; **inner** much less; inner surrounds the **stroma** (≈ matrix): enzymes, ATP made here by ATP synthase; own DNA, ribosomes, RNAs (p. 45)
- Inner membrane **not folded**, no electron-transport chain. The ETC, light-capturing systems and ATP synthase sit in the **thylakoid membrane (third membrane system)**; thylakoids stacked into **grana** (p. 46)
- **Two parts of photosynthesis** (p. 47):
- ↳ **Light reactions** (photosynthetic electron transfer): PS I + PS II → **NADPH + ATP**
- ↳ **Dark reactions** (carbon fixation): CO₂ → carbohydrates
- Products stored as **starch** or used for ATP in mitochondria; the two organelles cooperate (p. 48)
- **H⁺ gradient location** (p. 49): chloroplast → **inside the thylakoid** (large pH difference); mitochondrion → **crista/intermembrane space** (small pH difference). ATP made in the **stroma** / **matrix**

## Processes step by step | 2 min
### Signal hypothesis (protein into ER) (p. 14–15)
1. ER signal sequence emerges from the ribosome
2. SRP binds the signal + ribosome (translation pauses)
3. SRP docks on the SRP receptor on the rough ER
4. Ribosome binds the translocator; the chain threads through the pore
5. Signal peptidase cleaves the signal
6. Mature protein is released into the ER lumen

### Secretory pathway (p. 22–27)
ER (COP II vesicles) → cis Golgi → medial (glycosylation) → trans Golgi network (sorting) → secretory vesicles → exocytosis; COP I returns escaped proteins to the ER

### Chemiosmotic ATP production (p. 34–36, p. 44)
1. NADH (from the citric acid cycle) gives high-energy electrons to the ETC
2. Electron energy pumps H⁺ into the intermembrane space
3. Electrons + O₂ → H₂O
4. pH gradient + voltage = proton-motive force
5. H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase → ADP + Pi → ATP

### Endosymbiosis (p. 3–4)
Archaeal host → membrane invagination → nucleus → engulfs aerobic bacterium (mitochondrion) → later a photosynthetic bacterium (chloroplast) → genes transferred to the nucleus
