=== SLIDE 7 ===
Magma properties
Liquid Solid Gas
• Viscous fluid
• Most commonly silicate
composition
• Crystalizing crystals
• Residue after partial
melting
• Fragments assimilated
from the surrounding
rock (xenoliths)
• Bubbles filled with
volatile components
(H2O, CO2, H2S, Cl…)
• Form with pressure
decrease
• Cause of eruptions

Magma properties
Felsic Intermediate Ultramafic
• High SiO2, Na2O, K2O
• Low FeO, MgO, CaO
• High gas content
• Low density
• Low temperature
• High viscosity
• Highly explosive
• Key minerals: Quartz, K-
feldspar, Plagioclase, Mica
• Rhyolite, granite
• Lava domes
Mafic
• Average SiO2, Na2O, K2O
• Average FeO, MgO, CaO
• Average gas content
• Average density
• Average temperature
• Average viscosity
• Rather explosive
• Key minerals: Quartz,
Plagioclase, Amphibole
• Andesite, diorite
• Stratovolcanoes
• Low SiO2, Na2O, K2O
• High FeO, MgO, CaO
• Low gas content
• High density
• High temperature
• Low viscosity
• Effusive
• Key minerals: Plagioclase,
Amphibole, Pyroxene
• Basalt, gabbro
• Shield volcanoes
• Very low SiO2, Na2O, K2O
• Very high FeO, MgO, CaO
• Low gas content
• High density
• High temperature
• Very low viscosity
• Effusive
• Key minerals: Olivine,
Pyroxene

peridotite

=== SLIDE 14 ===
Igneous rocks
Plutonic rocks
• = intrusive
• Form when magma
„freezes“ (crystallizes)
beneath Earth surface
• Slow cooling
• Phaneritic or porphyritic
texture
=== SLIDE 15 ===
Igneous rocks
Volcanic rocks
• = extrusive
• Form when magma erupts
on surface
• Rapid cooling
• Aphanitic, glassy, vesicular
or porphyritic texture
• Explosive vs efusive
(depending on the magma
composition and gas
content)
=== SLIDE 16 ===
Igneous rocks
Pyroclastic rocks
• fragmented volcanic
material ejected and
deposited during explosive
eruptions
• Composed of volcanic ash,
lapilli, blocks, or bombs
• Clastic texture – made of
angular fragments (not
interlocking crystals)
=== SLIDE 17 ===
Igneous rocks
Pyroclastic rocks
• Ash (<2 mm) → forms tuff
• Lapilli (2–64 mm) → forms
lapilli tuff or lapillistone
• Blocks and bombs (>64
mm) → form agglomerate
or volcanic breccia

Igneous structures
Batholiths: are large
mass of intrusive
igneous that forms from
cooled magma deep in
the Earth's crust.
Batholiths are almost
always made mostly of
felsic or intermediate
rock types, such as
granite, quartz
monzonite, or diorite.
Half Dome,
Yosemite
National
Park, Sierra
Nevada
Batholith
Map of
North
American
batholiths
=== SLIDE 31 ===
Igneous structures
Stock: is a discordant
igneous intrusion having
a surface exposure of
less than 100 km2,
differing from batholiths
only in being smaller.
The term stock usually
refers to individual,
relatively small plutons
(<20 km diameter). Most
stocks are probably the
cupolas of hidden
batholith.
Syeniticstock,
southern Israel
=== SLIDE 32 ===
Igneous structures
Lopolith: a large igneous
intrusion which is
lenticular in shape with a
depressed central region.
Lopoliths are generally
concordant with the
intruded strata, with dike
or funnel-shaped feeder
bodies below the body.
Lopoliths can be huge in
size; the Bushveld
intrusive complex in
South Africa, for example,
has an area of about
66,000 square kilometers
and an exposed thickness
of 8 kilometers.
Magmatic
layering in the
Bushveld
complex
(lopolith),
South Africa
=== SLIDE 33 ===
Igneous structures
Laccolith: is a sheet
intrusion (or concordant
pluton) that has been
injected between two
layers of sedimentary
rock. The pressure of the
magma is high enough
that the overlying strata
are forced upward, giving
the laccolith a dome or
mushroom-like form with
a generally planar base.
Enchanted
Rock laccolith,
Texas
=== SLIDE 34 ===
Igneous structures
Sill: is a tabular sheet
intrusion that has
intruded between older
layers of sedimentary
rock, beds of volcanic
lava or tuff, or along the
direction of foliation in a
metamorphic rock. A sill
is a concordant intrusive
sheet, meaning that a sill
does not cut across
preexisting rock beds.
Basaltic sill,
Yellowstone
National Park
=== SLIDE 35 ===
Igneous structures
Dike or dyke: is a
discordant tabular
pluton; it forms from
magma that is forced
into cracks rather
than between beds of
sedimentary rock.
Pegmatite dykes
near Essex,
Connecticut
