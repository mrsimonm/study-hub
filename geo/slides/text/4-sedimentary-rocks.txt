=== SLIDE 6 ===
Weathering is the breakdown of rocks and minerals in place, at or near the
Earth’s surface, through physical, chemical, and biological processes.
Sedimentary processes - weathering
=== SLIDE 7 ===
Sedimentary processes - weathering
Mechanical weathering
• Breaks rocks into smaller pieces
without changing composition
• Processes:
• Frost wedging (expansion of
freezing water in cracks)
• Thermal expansion (insolation,
onionskin, in deserts or wildfires)
• Exfoliation (release of pressure)
• Biological activity (root
wedging, burrowing)
• Results: Angular, coarse
fragments, sharp edges
Frostwedging Rootwedging
Insolation(onionskin) Exfoliation
=== SLIDE 8 ===
Sedimentary processes - weathering
Chemical weathering
• Water + acids cause minerals to
dissolve or transform
• Happens faster in warm, wet
climates
• Keyreactions:
• Hydrolysis– feldspar→ clay +
ions
• Oxidation– iron minerals → rust
(hematite/goethite)
• Dissolution– calcite in rainwater
• Results: Softened rock, new
minerals (clay), ions in solution

Oxidation Dissolution

original location by natural agents such as water, wind, ice, or gravity.

=== SLIDE 13 ===
Transport is the movement of sediment from the site of erosion to the site of
deposition, by agents like water, wind, ice, or gravity.
Sedimentary processes - transport
=== SLIDE 14 ===
Sedimentary processes - transport
Sediment transport
• Follows after erosion
• Same agents as for erosion
(water, wind, glaciers, gravity)
• Keyprocesses:
• Sorting– Separation of grains by
size, shape, and density
• Rounding– Grains become
more rounded and smoothed
due to repeated abrasion
• Chemical alteration– Minerals
dissolve or transform
=== SLIDE 15 ===
Deposition is the process by which transported sediments come to rest and
accumulate in a new location, forming sedimentary layers.
Sedimentary processes - deposition
=== SLIDE 16 ===
Sedimentary processes - deposition
Sediment deposition
(sedimentation)
• End of transport stage
• Keycauses:
• Water slows down– e.g., lower
slope, river enters lake…
• Wind drops
• Glacier melts
• Gravity stops acting– e.g., base
of the cliff

Diagenesis includes all physical, chemical, and biological changes that occur
in sediments after deposition and before metamorphism.
Sedimentary processes - diagenesis

=== SLIDE 20 ===
Sedimentary processes - diagenesis
Sediment diagenesis
• Keyprocesses:
• Lithification– Making
sediment a rock (compaction
and cementation)
• Recrystalization– Crystals
reorganize or grow larger
• Authigenesis– Growth of
new minerals
• Dissolution– Unstable
minerals dissolve
• Biochemical alteration–
Alteration through microbial
activity

Depositional environments
=== SLIDE 22 ===
Fluvial:
Rivers and streams; well-
layered sands and muds,
moderate sorting.
Depositional environments - terrestrial
Alluvial fan:
Base of mountains; coarse,
poorly sorted sediments.
Glacial:
Ice-dominated; very
poorly sorted mix of all
grain sizes.
=== SLIDE 23 ===
Aeolian:
Wind-blown deserts; very
well-sorted, rounded sand
grains.
Depositional environments - terrestrial
Lacustrine:
Lakes; fine-grained, often
laminated sedimentary
beds.
Evaporitic:
Arid basins (Playa =
continental; Sabkha =
coastal); Crystalline evaporite
minerals, often bedded
=== SLIDE 24 ===
Deltaic:
River–sea interface;
alternating sand and mud
layers.
Depositional environments - transitional
Beach:
Wave-dominated coast;
well-sorted, rounded sand.
=== SLIDE 25 ===
Tidal flat:
Intertidal zone; alternating
mud and sand, ripple
marks.
Depositional environments - transitional
Lagoon:
Sheltered coastal area;
fine, quiet-water
sediments.
=== SLIDE 26 ===
Shallow marine:
Continental shelf and reef
areas; well-sorted sand
and carbonate mud.
Depositional environments - marine
Deep marine:
Abyssal plain; very fine-
grained muds and siliceous
or calcareous ooze.

Limestone
• Mineralogy: Primarily

• Texture: Mostly
microcrystalline, may
include fossils
• Color: Typically gray,
white, cream, or tan
• Depositional
environment: Warm,
shallow marine settings
(reefs, carbonate
platforms), lakes, or
tidal flats
=== SLIDE 48 ===
Sedimentary rocks
Dolostone
• Mineralogy: Primarily
dolomite (CaMg(CO₃)₂)
• Texture: Usually
crystalline; may
preserve textures of
original limestone
• Color: Commonly gray,
brown, pink, or beige
• Depositional
environment: Shallow
marine settings, tidal
flats, sabkhas, or areas
of secondary alteration
of limestone
