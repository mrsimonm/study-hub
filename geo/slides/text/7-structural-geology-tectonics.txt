Principles of deformation
Deformation and internal strength
• Deformation is a general term
describing any change in a rock’s
shape, position, or orientation
due to stress.
• Internal strength is the ability of
a rock to resist deformation under
stress, controlled by its
composition, temperature,
pressure, and strain rate.
Stress and strain
• Stress is the force exerted per
unit area
• Strain is the physical change
that results in response to that
force.
=== SLIDE 8 ===
Principles of deformation
When applied stress is greater
than the internal strength of
rock, strain results in the form
of deformation of the rock.
=== SLIDE 9 ===
Principles of deformation
Types of stress:
• Tensional: pulling in
opposite directions
• Compressional: pushing
together
• Shear: transverse forces
=== SLIDE 10 ===
Principles of deformation
Types of strain:
• Elastic: reversible after a
stress is released
• Ductile: material changes
its shape permanently
• Brittle: material fractures

• High temperature->

• High strain rate-> brittle
• Fluids

=== SLIDE 12 ===
Deformational structures
Brittle deformation
• Joints: Planar fractures
along which there has been
no visible displacement.
• Faults: Planar fractures
along which displacement
has occurred.
• Veins: Fracture fillings
formed when minerals
precipitate from fluids in
open cracks.
=== SLIDE 13 ===
Deformational structures
Typical conditions of brittle
deformation
• Depth: 0–15 km
• Temperature: <~300 °C
• Pressure: Low confining
pressure
• Strain rate: Higher strain
rate favors brittle behavior
• Result: Fracturing, faulting

Deformational structures
Fault description
• Fault plane: plane of
displacement
• Hanging wall: upper block
• Foot wall: lower block
• Fault line: where the fault
plane intersects surface
• Fault scarp: a steep slope
or cliff on the surface
created by a fault
=== SLIDE 16 ===
Deformational structures
Types of faults
• Normal fault: extension,
hanging wall move down
• Reverse fault: compression,
hanging wall moves up
• Thrust fault: compression,
hanging wall moves up but
under low angle
• Strike-slip fault: shear
stress, blocks move laterally

Tectonics
Tectonic (lithospheric) plates
• The lithosphere is broken
into a mosaic of rigid plates.
• Each plate behaves as a rigid
block internally, but
deformation concentrates
along plate boundaries.

=== SLIDE 28 ===
Earth structure–internal processes
Mantle convection
Mantle rocks heat near the core
➢ Increases volume and decreases density
➢ Rises to the surface
➢ There it cools and sinks again
Rigid lithospheric plates
“float” on a viscous
asthenosphere.
=== SLIDE 29 ===
Tectonics
Plate Boundaries
• Sites of deformation,
earthquakes, and magmatism
1. Divergent boundaries:
plates move apart →crustal

2. Convergent boundaries:
plates converge, collide →

3. Transform boundaries:
plates slide past each other

=== SLIDE 30 ===
Tectonics

• Melting of upper mantle →

• Foramtion of normal faults,
mid-ocean ridges and rift
valleys
