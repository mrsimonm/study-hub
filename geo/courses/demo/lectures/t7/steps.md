## What hardness is | 4 min

:::idea The idea
**Hardness** is how well a mineral resists being **scratched**. A harder mineral scratches a softer one, never the other way round.
:::

To test it, rub one mineral against another and see which one leaves a scratch.

- If A scratches B, then A is harder than B.
- If neither scratches the other, they have about the same hardness.

## The ten reference minerals | 6 min

In 1812 Friedrich Mohs picked ten minerals as a ruler. Number **1 is the softest**, number **10 the hardest**.

| Number | Mineral | Number | Mineral |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Talc | 6 | Orthoclase |
| 2 | Gypsum | 7 | Quartz |
| 3 | Calcite | 8 | Topaz |
| 4 | Fluorite | 9 | Corundum |
| 5 | Apatite | 10 | Diamond |

:::trap Exam trap
The Mohs scale is a **ranking**, not a measurement with equal steps. Diamond (10) is much harder than corundum (9).
:::

:::ana Analogy
Think of a race result: first, second, third. It tells you the order, but not whether the winner was a second ahead or a minute ahead.
:::

## Quick field tests | 5 min

You do not always have the ten minerals with you. Everyday objects work as rough rulers:

| Object | Hardness |
|---|---|
| Fingernail | about 2.5 |
| Copper coin | about 3.5 |
| Window glass or steel knife | about 5.5 |

### Other helpful properties

- **Streak**: the colour of the powder on unglazed porcelain.
- **Lustre**: how the surface reflects light.
- **Cleavage** and **fracture**: how the mineral breaks.
